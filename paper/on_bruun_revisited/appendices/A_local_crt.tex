\section{Expanded Local CRT Algebra}
\label{app:crt}

For completeness, the trinomial merge can be checked without invoking an
abstract CRT theorem.  Put $u=z^{h/2}$.  Modulo $p_+$,
\begin{equation}
 u^2+bu+1=0,\qquad u^{-1}=-(u+b).
 \label{eq:crt-u-inverse}
\end{equation}
Since $p_-=p_+-2bu$, one has
\begin{equation}
 p_-{u+b\over2b}\equiv-u(u+b)=1\pmod{p_+}.
 \label{eq:crt-direct-check}
\end{equation}
Thus the correction from $r_-$ to $r_+$ is the child difference multiplied by
the inverse in \eqref{eq:crt-direct-check}.  Degree reduction modulo $p_+$ makes
$\deg t<h$, and multiplication by $p_-$ gives a representative of degree below
$2h$.  This is exactly the unique parent coordinate range.

For binomial siblings, $p_-=z^h-1\equiv-2\pmod{p_+}$.  The correction equation
$r_-+p_-t\equiv r_+$ gives $t=(r_--r_+)/2$.  In normalized coordinates, every
four-real pair merge has inverse $T_\theta^{\trans}/2$.  Across the complete
unbalanced tree, these local measures combine with the terminal pair weights
to give the global inverse $B_N^{-1}=B_N^{\trans}/N$ proved from Fourier
evaluation in Section~\ref{sec:fourier-identity}.
