\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Bruun's real factor tree supports a complete Fourier theory.  Explicit local
CRT interpolation makes the tree two-sided.  The quotient generator
$e_\theta$ gives every conjugate pair a unit Euclidean coordinate, turning each
node into a scaled-orthogonal rotation and Hadamard map.  Evaluation at the
terminal roots then proves equality with the packed DFT, not merely invertibility
or numerical agreement.  The same exact operator admits DIF, DIT, and diagonal
phase-packet walks; the factors of $z^N+1$ give an odd-frequency companion; and
positive-cone gauges give conservative transport and exact equilibrium
realizations in the declared ideal linear categories.  A remaining algebraic
problem is to classify all sparse real CRT
trees that are simultaneously scaled orthogonal and closed under a finite
family of local coordinate types.
