\begin{abstract}
Bruun's 1978 real-coefficient Fourier factorization is revisited as a complete
algebraic transform rather than only a forward reduction scheme.  The starting
point is the factor tree of $z^N-1$ into a binomial spine and real conjugate-pair
trinomials.  An explicit Chinese-remainder merge supplies a two-sided inverse
at every internal node.  A normalized coordinate
$e_\theta=(z-\cos\theta)/\sin\theta$ turns every terminal quadratic quotient
into a Euclidean complex line with $e_\theta^2=-1$.  In these coordinates each
nontrivial node is a plane rotation followed by a Hadamard map.  Consequently,
the packed real transform $B_N$ satisfies $B_N^{\trans}B_N=N I$, and direct
evaluation in the terminal quotients proves that $B_N$ is exactly the discrete
Fourier transform up to an explicit output permutation.  The same factorization
admits decimation-in-frequency, decimation-in-time, and diagonal phase-packet
walks.  Its half-bin companion gives an exact odd-frequency transform using
real two-plane arithmetic.  Finally, a positive-cone lift and two diagonal
gauges give conservative transport and equilibrium realizations whose signed
projection is exactly the Fourier map.  Symbolic examples and binary64 audits
are reported separately from the proofs.  The resulting theory identifies an
exact, condition-one, real arithmetic Fourier factorization and a physically
realizable representative of it in the declared ideal linear categories.
\end{abstract}
