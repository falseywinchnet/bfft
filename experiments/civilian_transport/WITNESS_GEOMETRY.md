# Where a locally supported correction can be identified

This experiment borrows an observation from BTB: local restoration can be a
more constrained question than complete reconstruction. It does not implement
BTB, require an iterative denoiser, or use fixed-point convergence as evidence
of recovered structure. The earlier Gaussian proximal-iteration diagnostic is
not part of this construction.

The proposed question is: can independent observations support a reduction in
clean error along correction directions suggested by the current representation?
This is distinct from identifying the physical cause of every residual or
certifying the same correction at an unobserved future time.

## 1. Learning directions without using their witnesses

Split the acquired history into an initial prefix, training observations T,
and untouched witness observations V. In the experiment the prefix is 0–4 s,
T is 4.2,4.4,...,6.0 s, and V is 4.1,4.3,...,5.9 s. This is a history-based
smoothing diagnostic evaluated at 6 s. It is not a zero-latency decision made
before those witnesses arrive.

Fit the existing relational-current Kalman model on the prefix. Call its mean
field f0(t). As training observations arrive, retain the entire change in the
current mean induced by each observation:

    d_i(t) = L_t (m_i - m_(i-1)).

Each d_i is a compatible integrated current, and their sum is the complete
training-only correction. The directions depend on noisy training values;
they are not assumed clean, orthogonal, or physically correct. The existing
Matérn current prior supplies candidate relationships, not the certificate.
The witness values are never used to construct or choose the direction space.

All linear combinations remain in the same finite current representation.
The experiment tests both one ray (scaling the complete proposed correction)
and the continuous span of the individual observation-induced directions.
It does not introduce a catalog of maneuver types or choose a length scale
using clean future values.

## 2. Exact witness model and scope

Let f denote the unknown clean positions at witness times, stacked across
space and time. Assume

    y_V = f + epsilon,
    epsilon ~ N(0, sigma^2 I), independent of the training measurements.

The signal f can be arbitrary. There is no assumption that it was generated by
the current prior, belongs to the correction span, or is globally smooth.
Known sigma and independence/zero mean are substantive sensor assumptions.
A correlated sensor drift violates this contract unless separately modeled.

Condition on the training record. Both f0 and the witness direction matrix
D=[d_1(V),...,d_k(V)] are now fixed. Let

    D/sigma = U diag(s) V^T

be its rank-r SVD with only nonzero singular directions retained. Define

    v = U^T (y_V-f0)/sigma,
    a = U^T (f-f0)/sigma.

Then v-a~N(0,I_r). Therefore, for c=sqrt(chi2_r(1-alpha)),

    P(||v-a|| <= c | training, fixed clean path) = 1-alpha.

The subspace and its rank may be chosen from training data; they may not be
selected using the witnesses without accounting for that selection. Numerical
rank uses a machine-precision tolerance, not an accuracy-tuned threshold.

## 3. A finite correction from confidence geometry

A correction with independent witness coordinates z has witness displacement
sigma U z. Its reduction in summed CLEAN squared position error is

    G(z) = ||f-f0||^2 - ||f-f0-sigma U z||^2
         = sigma^2 (2 z^T a - ||z||^2).

The part of f-f0 orthogonal to the correction space cancels from this identity.
On the single confidence event above, simultaneously for every z,

    G(z) >= sigma^2 (2 z^T v - ||z||^2 - 2 c ||z||).

This simultaneous statement is why choosing z after seeing the witnesses does
not invalidate the bound. By Cauchy–Schwarz the lower bound is maximized by
aligning z with v; maximizing its scalar radius gives the explicit answer

    z_hat = (1-c/||v||)_+ v,
    certified lower gain = sigma^2 (||v||-c)_+^2.

If the projected evidence does not exceed its uncertainty radius, the answer
is zero. Otherwise, its magnitude is shortened by that radius. This is a
single algebraic confidence calculation. There is no denoising iteration,
repeated likelihood, or claim that a small numerical update proves correctness.

This is a frequentist, per-record confidence statement, not an assertion that
every accepted correction must improve the truth. A 95% procedure can fail on
5% of records under its own assumptions. Its exact null behavior is testable.

A one-direction version uses the score s=d^T(y_V-f0), uncertainty
c1=Phi^-1(1-alpha)*sigma*||d||, and scale

    eta = clip((s-c1)/||d||^2, 0, 1).

The lower gain eta*(2*(s-c1)-eta*||d||^2) holds simultaneously for all nonnegative
scales on the one-sided confidence event. The ray and span are separate
procedures at their stated confidence levels, not a jointly selected winner.

## 3a. The geometric statement is more general than Gaussian shrinkage

Let K be any nonempty closed convex confidence region for the projected noise
v-a. On the event v-a in K, the clean improvement satisfies

    G(z)/sigma^2 >= 2 z^T v - ||z||^2 - 2 h_K(z),

where h_K is the support function. The maximizing correction has a geometric
expression:

    z_hat = v - projection_K(v).

To verify, let p=projection_K(v). The projection inequality says
(v-p)^T(k-p)<=0 for every k in K, hence h_K(v-p)=(v-p)^T p.
Substitution gives a lower gain ||v-p||^2. For any competing z, h_K(z)>=z^T p,
so its lower gain is at most 2 z^T(v-p)-||z||^2<=||v-p||^2.
The ball formula above is this result with K a radius-c Euclidean ball.

The supported correction is therefore the component outside the noise
confidence region. Its direction is normal to that region at the projection.
This supplies an observation-defined notion of a locally justified correction;
it does not require knowing an entire clean-motion manifold in advance.
The existence and calibration of K are still required. Inventing K or fitting
it to the same residual without accounting for selection would lose the claim.
No non-Gaussian empirical result is claimed by this extension of the proof.

## 4. Transporting the correction exposes another gap

At a query t the same coefficients produce

    delta(t) = E(t) z_hat,
    E(t) = D(t) V diag(1/s).

Thus a locally supported correction is an actual change in the represented
current, not a disconnected adjustment to witness pixels. However, this does
not prove it improves the unknown clean position at t. Two problems remain:

1. A weakly observed direction can have a large query response. The operator
   norm ||E(t)||/sigma measures amplification of witness displacement into the
   query. SVD singular values and the measured amplification are retained.
2. A direction in ker D(V) can have nonzero D(t). Witnesses supply no constraint
   on that direction. Choosing zero as the returned correction does not remove
   its possible physical effect or establish zero uncertainty.

Even when D(V) has full column rank, the actual future may lie outside the
relationships proposed by D. A confidence region for the projected witness
residual is not a calibrated posterior or a confidence region for that future.
The experiment therefore reports clean witness error and forecast error
separately. It does not attach the old Kalman covariance to the altered mean
and present that pair as a new posterior.

## 5. Why this addresses one part of the original gap

The prior proposes a possible way of changing the current. Independent evidence
now checks whether the correction removes error at untouched positions, with a
bound that does not assume the prior is the truth. This separates the source
of a proposed relationship from evidence supporting its local effect.

Zak can express the current and its uncertain relationships, but an invertible
change of current coordinates leaves D(V), the witness geometry, and the
certificate unchanged. Treating multiple representations as fresh witnesses
would be invalid.

The Ghost of Newton concern becomes a concrete control: build the direction
from noise and then reuse that noise as the witness. Such an apparent success
has no independence guarantee. The study compares this invalid reuse against
untouched witnesses, including an exact null experiment.

## 6. An indistinguishable pair

The study includes two worlds:

    Motion change:  truth = base + g, sensor error = epsilon.
    Sensor drift:   truth = base,     sensor error = g + epsilon.

Their observed histories are exactly the same: base+g+epsilon. No function of
those observations can return different answers for the two worlds. Independent
random sensor noise does not resolve an unknown coherent drift g. At 8 s the
two truths differ by more than 6 m in the retained fixture design.

Under the motion interpretation the witness noise contract holds. Under the
drift interpretation it fails. A certificate that supports following g under
the first interpretation cannot establish that g is physical motion. This is
an identifiability obstruction, not a deficiency repaired by more refinement.

## 6a. Coherent nuisance creates neutral directions

If the projected sensor error may contain any vector in a known nuisance
subspace N, a suitable confidence region can be an unbounded cylinder

    K = N + {u in N^perp : ||u|| <= c}.

Its projection absorbs the entire N component of v. Thus z_hat has zero
component along N, and only the N^perp residual can yield a guaranteed
correction. For a projected isotropic Gaussian remainder, c can be calibrated
with the chi-square dimension of N^perp. This is a mathematical statement for
a declared nuisance subspace, not evidence that the benchmark drift subspace
was known to the estimator.

If a physical motion change and a sensor change occupy the same observational
direction, that component remains neutral under this guarantee. A Kalman
architecture should retain its state/nuisance ambiguity rather than shrink its
uncertainty through repeated explanation of the same measurements. Identifying
that component requires an additional restriction or observation that separates
the two sources. Restricting N is substantive source/sensor information.

## 7. Experiment status

This is a finite local-risk construction and a diagnostic integration with the
current Kalman representation. It is not promoted as a replacement tracker.
The held-out witnesses are consumed once for support assessment; corrected
fields are terminal outputs of this experiment, not fed back as new independent
measurements. A future online architecture must retain that evidence lineage
and obtain a justified model for continuation and correlated sensor error.
