The mathematics behind the motion +

Two rails carry a signed sample

A fixed-pivot input rocker sets p = (1 + x) / 2 and n = (1 − x) / 2 through its two opposite endpoint pins. Both shuttles remain between 0 and 1; their separation p − n is the sample. Negative weights exchange the rails.

Geometry performs each operation

The eight-point Bruun factorization is lifted to two rails and given a forward kinematic gauge. Each output becomes a convex average, made by a bar with a fixed tap: q = (1 − β)a + βb.

The 44 bars use just two tap fractions: 36 at ½, and 8 at √2 − 1. Output ruler calibration removes the fixed gauge and converts the orthonormal coordinates to the ordinary FFT scale.

A finite-motion model

For a unit bar, the endpoint heights are 0.55a and 0.55b. Their horizontal separation is √(1 − 0.55²(b − a)²). Its length therefore stays exactly one throughout the slider range. The fixed tap’s vertical coordinate is the required average, even at finite rotation.

What this simulation represents

An ideal, rigid, frictionless linkage at quasistatic equilibrium. Animation moves the prescribed inputs; all output positions satisfy the linkage constraints in every frame. Each colored branched member is one rigid yoke translating in a vertical guide. Its source slot and every receiving slot move together. Long pin axles connect the front levers to yokes in separate depth layers; overlapping members in this front projection are not joined. The bottom ruler travels with the negative rail, while its pointer travels with the positive rail: their relative displacement is the Fourier coordinate.

This is a layered ideal kinematic assembly. The model checks connected pin-and-slot interfaces and rigid member motion. Finite-thickness axle clearances, friction, inertia and fabrication tolerances still require an engineering layout.
