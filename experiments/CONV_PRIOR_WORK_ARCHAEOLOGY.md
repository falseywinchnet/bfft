# Prior-work archaeology for CONV

This note separates direct precedent, theorem support, and adjacent technique.
It is not proposed paper text.

## Strong citations

### Toponogov: minimizing Eikonal characteristics

V. A. Toponogov, *Theorems on Shortest Paths in Noncompact Riemannian
Spaces of Positive Curvature* (1970), proves that on a complete noncompact
Riemannian manifold with strictly positive sectional curvature
\(0<K\le k_1\), every geodesic segment of length at most
\(\pi/\sqrt{k_1}\) is globally minimizing.  For nonnegative curvature it
proves only existence of an unspecified positive minimizing radius.

Use: conditional support for a curvature-limited Eikonal transport radius.
Do not claim applicability to a finite raster until a complete extension with
the required curvature bounds is constructed.

Source: https://www.sovietrxiv.org/items/ru-197001.37368

### Mirebeau: anisotropic Eikonal numerics

J.-M. Mirebeau, *Anisotropic Fast-Marching on Cartesian Grids Using Lattice
Basis Reduction* and *On the Accuracy of Anisotropic Fast Marching*, formulate
and analyze fast marching for generalized Eikonal equations generated by
continuous Riemannian metrics, including pronounced anisotropy.

Use: direct support for the numerical realization of an SPD-metric Eikonal
layer on Cartesian samples.  This supports the solver, not CONV's interpolation
law.

Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.1546 and
https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.5300

### Schoenberg/total positivity lineage

Modern summaries state the classical equivalence between total positivity and
variation diminution and cite Schoenberg, Karlin, de Boor, and Pinkus.  Recent
work on positive B-spline Wronskians explicitly connects total positivity to
Hermite interpolation.

Use: historical and mathematical context for variation-diminishing bases and
Hermite/Bernstein representations.  CONV is nonlinear and signed-fibre
projected, so it must not be described as an instance of a totally positive
linear matrix.

Sources:

- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44146-026-00264-3
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10092-024-00613-0
- https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-94-015-8674-0

### Harten: cell averages, prediction, and details

Harten's multiresolution framework represents fine data by coarse grid
averages and prediction errors.  Later work explicitly treats prediction and
decimation as compatible operators and develops cell-average reconstruction.

Use: mandatory prior art for CONV's conservative multiresolution analysis.
The novelty cannot be "cell averages plus residual details."  The relevant
distinction is that CONV uses its ordered-current interpolant as predictor and
proves a zero-detail variation law.

Sources:

- https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0733060
- https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03127
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10444-022-09997-5

## Useful Soviet interpolation history

### Yanenko and Kvasov: multidimensional spline construction

N. N. Yanenko and B. I. Kvasov, *An Iterative Method for Constructing
Polycubic Spline Functions* (1970), formulate one- and multidimensional spline
interpolation as fourth-order boundary-value problems whose coefficient
vanishes at interpolation nodes.  They use finite differences, fractional
steps, and stabilizing operators on rectangular and irregular grids.

Use: a valuable historical citation showing that multidimensional nodal spline
interpolation and non-equivalence to tensor bicubic splines were explicitly
studied in the Soviet numerical-analysis literature.  It is not direct
precedent for CONV: their method is global and iterative, while CONV is compact,
direct, and projects signed currents.

Source: https://www.sovietrxiv.org/items/ru-197001.12984

### Freud: approximation of an antiderivative from derivative approximation

G. Freud, *On Weighted Approximation by Polynomials on the Real Axis* (1970),
includes an inverse-type estimate relating approximation of an antiderivative
to approximation of its derivative, including bounded-variation data.

Use: mathematically adjacent to CONV's representation of a profile as the
integral of a differential current.  It is too remote to cite as algorithmic
precedent, but may support an approximation-theoretic discussion of integrated
current error.

Source: https://sovietrxiv.org/items/ru-197001.56750

### Korneichuk: Bernstein-Rogozinskii approximation

The archive exposes an erratum referring to N. P. Korneichuk's 1959 paper on
periodic Lipschitz approximation by Bernstein-Rogozinskii sums.  The original
paper should be recovered before citation; an erratum alone is insufficient.

Archive pointer: https://sovietrxiv.org/items/ru-196001.31449

## Metric construction and image geometry

### Structure tensor interpolation

Baghaie and Yu compute a structure tensor, classify local geometry by its
eigenvalues, and interpolate along its tangent eigenvector.  This is direct
image-interpolation prior art for extracting orientation from measured image
structure.

Use: cite when positioning CONV's tensor-derived two-dimensional geometry.
The difference is that CONV does not switch among content classes and uses a
single convex factor-order law.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.5564

### Riemannian color structure tensor

Recent Riemannian color-geometry work defines
\(s_{ij}=g_{\mu\nu}u^\mu_{,i}u^\nu_{,j}\), exactly the multichannel quadratic
current/structure tensor form, and uses its eigenstructure for anisotropic
diffusion.

Use: strong support for the multichannel SPD tensor construction, but not for
CONV's determinant normalization or interpolation theorem.

Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10851-024-01223-9

### Locally adaptive frames

Gauge-frame work derives local frames from structure tensors or Hessians and
documents their failure at crossings, bifurcations, and isotropic regions.

Use: supports CONV's sign-free tensor interpolation and symmetric isotropic
fallback rather than direct interpolation of eigenvector signs.

Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10851-016-0641-0

## Metric extension and boundary handling

### Smooth boundaryless extension

Ballmann, Polymerakis, and collaborators prove that a Riemannian manifold with
boundary embeds isometrically into a same-dimensional manifold without
boundary.  Their construction attaches an infinite collar and extends the SPD
metric smoothly using local frames and a partition of unity.

Use: this supplies a concrete route for extending a raster-domain metric past
its boundary.

Limitation: the construction does not, by itself, prove completeness,
noncompact positive curvature, determinant-one normalization, or preservation
of a curvature bound.  It therefore does not automatically unlock Toponogov's
theorem for CONV.

Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12220-019-00196-1

### Working directly with complete manifolds with boundary

Bianchi, Güneysu, and Setti develop Neumann cutoffs and essential
self-adjointness for complete Riemannian manifolds with boundary.

Use: supports a Neumann Laplace-Beltrami/Green construction without erasing the
boundary.  It does not establish Toponogov's positive-curvature minimizing
radius.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11120

### Boundary distance geometry

Ivanov proves recovery results for complete Riemannian manifolds with
non-convex boundary using distance-difference data, without imposing boundary
convexity.

Use: evidence that boundary distance geometry can be treated intrinsically;
adjacent rather than direct support for CONV.

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13153

## Bernstein integration and Gram geometry

Work on Bernstein Gram matrices and iterated Bernstein approximation provides
formal context for exact integration, positivity, and shape preservation.

Use: supporting citations for the Bernstein representation and exact Gram
metric.  These papers do not supply CONV's signed-current admission.

Sources:

- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11075-022-01284-0
- https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.0684
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.08692

## Recommended additions

The strongest additions to the bibliography are:

1. Toponogov for the conditional minimizing-geodesic radius;
2. Mirebeau for anisotropic Cartesian Eikonal computation;
3. Schoenberg/de Boor/Karlin lineage for variation diminution and total
   positivity;
4. Harten for coarse averages, prediction errors, and multiresolution;
5. Yanenko and Kvasov for historical multidimensional spline construction;
6. the structure-tensor and gauge-frame papers for image-derived SPD geometry;
7. the boundaryless-extension proposition if CONV explicitly constructs such
   an extension.

The extension proposition should not be cited as if it preserves positive
curvature.  That remains an unsolved obligation for applying Toponogov directly
to the raster metric.
