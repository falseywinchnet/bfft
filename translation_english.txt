Good evening. It is a privilege to be here tonight in the Krayot. What is this, Kiryat Shmuel here? Thank God.

We had a very unusual journey on the way here today. I felt as though it was the Exodus from Egypt. Something strange happened: we filled the car’s tank, and suddenly the fuel light came on—there was no fuel. I do not know; there must have been a leak. Suddenly we were stranded on a highway, Route 6. The car died. By a miracle, with the fumes that were left, we managed to get off the road. It was good that we did not get stuck in the middle of the road. We got off, and it was good there was a shoulder too. That too is a miracle, because there are places with no shoulder.

As soon as we got stuck there, I looked at the bushes and saw a jerrycan. A jerrycan! I said, “What is this?” It said Dor Alon on it. I said, “The question is whether it has fuel inside.” That would be quite a story. I opened it; I could smell a little fuel inside, and there were drops too. In short, we were there calling Didi, calling the tow truck—nobody came. A policeman arrived and said, “I’ll send you a patrol car.” No response. Finally he said, “All right, I’ll have to walk several kilometers with the jerrycan to find a station.” He started walking. Then some Breslov Hasid came by; he needed to make a phone call and happened to stop beside me. I told him, “Listen, do you see him? Go straight on—you’ll see him walking there with a jerrycan in his hand. Take him to a gas station.” Miracle after miracle.

So the man took him. Afterward, finding someone to get back from there—this is a highway, not a street; there is no address. In short, we got through it. He brought back the jerrycan, we filled up, and started driving. Ten minutes later the warning light came on again. We reached a gas station on the very last meter. But we had several miracles. Think about it: if there had not been a jerrycan, what would we have done? The car stopped on a 100-kilometer highway at the one place where someone had thrown a jerrycan. Where do you find a ready jerrycan at the side of the road? We put fuel in it. One would have to be blind not to see the hand of the Holy One, blessed be He. It is simply unbelievable.

Thank God, apparently there is going to be a powerful sermon today. Usually, when Satan resists, you know something is going to happen that will affect people in the future.

This past Shabbat we finished the portion of Shoftim. We are entering Ki Tetze, and afterward we move to Ki Tavo. We are approaching the end—approaching the run-up to Simchat Torah: Vayelech, Ha’azinu, and Vezot Haberakhah.

As is known, Moses wrote the Book of Deuteronomy—or, more accurately, spoke it—over thirty-six days. “Eleh ha-devarim” (“These are the words”) has the numerical value of thirty-six. There are eleven portions, from Deuteronomy through Vezot Haberakhah. This is essentially Moses our teacher’s life-address. He is 120 years old; it is the final month of his life. “His eye had not dimmed,” as it is written. He could still see. He did not need reading glasses or distance glasses. He functioned; his mind was clear.

One of the biggest problems of old age is not only that the body withers—the knees, ankles, back, everything; the eyes and hearing, everything deteriorates. The most dangerous thing is when the mind deteriorates: when a person does not remember the names of his children or grandchildren. He has flashes of recognition: suddenly, “I know you.” He tells his son, “Have we met before?” Every few minutes they must tell him the same thing, and each time he is newly moved because he has forgotten that they told him only minutes earlier. It is terrible to reach such a state.

I have a friend like that. He told me, “I think it has already started with me—Alzheimer’s. I’m afraid of where it is going.” In short, we need to pray. We need to pray that when we are old, the Holy One, blessed be He, will have mercy on us and we will not have this illness. Why? Because those who suffer most from it are the people around the patient. The person who has it does not feel it so much, because, as I told you, he is in his own world. The ones who suffer are the people around him. They come to see him and he says, “I don’t know who you are.” It is terrible.

And if, for example, that person has hidden money, accounts abroad, all kinds of things—Bitcoin, whatever—in one instant it all disappears. He no longer remembers the passwords; he does not even know where to look. It can happen suddenly: you do not remember, you try to remember, and it is all gone. So Moses, at the very end of his life, gives this address.

I always say that our generation is very, very, very low in terms of its general level. True, there are more than a million people described as penitents, but I mean the general level. When I started giving lectures thirty-two years ago, the secular people then were at least ten levels higher than secular people today. Today there is nothing to discuss with a secular person; you cannot develop a conversation with him. His mind is not focused, he has attention and concentration problems; sixty or seventy percent of them are on drugs, cannabis, and their minds do not function at all. They cannot keep their minds on anything. They sit at your table, and after three or four minutes at a meal they get up, leave, and return. They cannot sit. The state of the generation today is terrible.

So to come to such people and give them a philosophical talk—to prove that the Torah comes from the Creator and what that means for life—is ten times harder. It is not what it used to be. You used to come to a home gathering, show four or five proofs, and the secular people would argue, there would be battles, but when you spoke to the point they understood. Today, you see what interests the youth; in practice there is almost nobody to speak to. So when you suddenly find a boy or girl with a head on their shoulders, who is clear-minded, it is a miracle. The situation is terrible.

There is another problem today: people hate rebuke. You cannot speak truthfully about what is written in the Torah. The preachers here are afraid. Look: in Israel there may be a thousand preachers. How many speak forcefully? Not even one—perhaps two out of a thousand, to be safe. Two who speak forcefully about punishment, judgment, what will happen to the wicked in the World to Come, and what it means to desecrate Shabbat. Nobody speaks about it. Ask them, “Why do you not speak?” “Oh, leave it; people are put off. We are afraid of driving them away”—all kinds of excuses. They are afraid to speak.

That too is Satan’s counsel. The only way today to bring people closer to Torah is for a person to understand the price he will pay for his actions. Without that there is no progress—none. I will give you proof of it.

When I enlisted in the army, they sent me to the technical base here. It is probably near here—the Technion? It was in the same area, where the refineries come out of those towers. I spent six months there before they sent me to a squadron. When I was at the technical base, from the moment I arrived I had tremendous chemistry with my NCO, a guy from the Krayot named Elimar. He must be close to sixty today. This Elimar was my commander. I had just arrived, and immediately there was a click between us; we instantly became friends. He was my commander. What did that mean? Perks. I did not run; I did not clean the kitchen or the toilets. I sat playing backgammon with him for half the day in air-conditioning. He sent me home every Shabbat. I brought a kitbag full of food, and he and I would spread out feasts while all the soldiers were running around doing duties. For the first two or three weeks, he and I were at summer camp. He gave them orders while he sat talking and laughing with me.

One day there was not a soldier to be found. They had to send someone on an urgent mission, and I was the only one there. Uncomfortably he said, “Listen, Mizrahi, I have no choice. I have to send you on a mission. There is nobody else to send.” It was as though he was apologizing to me. The commander sent me on a mission, and I did it—how do they say it in Israel?—like his face; terribly. When I came back, after an hour or two he discovered that the job had not been performed one hundred percent properly. He came to me with an angry face and said, “Mizrahi, know that you disappointed me badly. After everything I did for you—I exempted you from all the duties—the one time I needed you, you disappointed me badly.”

Why do I remember that story now? You can learn a great moral lesson from it. Listen to the lesson. I loved this Elimar dearly. He loved me and I loved him. Let us say, by analogy, that he is the Holy One, blessed be He, and I am the person. He is now my boss. I love my boss; I love the Holy One, blessed be He, with all my soul. I fulfill what is written in the Torah: “You shall love the Lord your God.” There is no doubt that I loved him and appreciated him one hundred percent; I was one hundred percent grateful to him. The proof is that every Sunday I would lug in a bag full of food so that he would have good food. He had to stay on base for Shabbat, so I came with a bag full of things and brought them to him. There was no question that I did not appreciate him, or that he had not been good to me. I loved him in every possible way.

So why did I not obey him the one time he assigned me a task? Because I had no awe of him; I had no fear of him. I said to myself, “What is he going to do? Give me a little slap and say, ‘Next time, try harder’? It will be all right. We will cross that bridge when we get to it.” Because I had no fear of him, I did not carry out the mission.

Many people love the Holy One, blessed be He—or at least imagine that they do. They feel that they love Him, but they do not obey Him at all: every five minutes, transgressions; sins punishable by karet; evil speech; non-kosher foods; Shabbat desecration; harming people; cheating people in business. How can that be? He kisses a mezuzah. He keeps Psalms in his sock, or whatever; he sees a rabbi and runs to kiss his beard. He comes to synagogue and immediately goes to the Ark as though he were at the Western Wall. He is not an atheist or a hater of religion. We are talking about a traditional Jew; perhaps his parents are even ultra-Orthodox. You cannot say that he does not love God.

He is not like the God-haters on Balfour Street who would be prepared to die in order to blaspheme the Torah—like that vile, self-described “proud” leftist [name unclear in the source], a cursed man. I do not know whether there are even ten such cursed people in the country. Thank God, in this confrontation his career ended. He was supposed to enter the Knesset and do great damage to yeshivot and religion. Why is that a relief to me? Because he is not merely left-wing; he is actively and maliciously left-wing. There are many leftists who are only good at talking. He is vile and active. He raises millions from companies. He said that he has twenty thousand donors from business leaders who flood him with money so that he can turn religious people away from their faith. Anyone who leaves religion, he buys them a laptop and a new iPhone as a gift. That is thousands of dollars. Anyone who comes and says, “I am leaving religion,” he gives a laptop, gives an iPhone, runs to arrange a job and somewhere to live.

I said that sometimes one has to learn moral lessons from the dirtiest and worst people in the world; one can learn from them too. What can we learn from such a person? It is written concerning “Baruch She’amar” that a note descended from heaven. Who wrote “Baruch She’amar”? The Sages say a note descended from heaven. Here, a note has descended from heaven. What does this man do? He raises money and goes to turn people away from their religion. He sacrifices himself to take people who keep the commandments and turn them into Gentiles. For what? Nothing. He does not believe in the World to Come.

It is not like us. We come, give lectures, want to bring merit to the public, arrange for preachers to come, hold seminars. Why? We know that we do not work for nothing. Let us not be hypocrites: if there were no reward for it, it is doubtful that people would do public outreach at all. Most outreach is because of the tremendous things written about it in the Talmud and the Zohar. About one who brings merit to the many, things are written that are not even written about the prophets. Even in Duties of the Heart it says that one who brings people back in repentance has a rank greater than that of the wholly righteous prophets. Do you hear such a thing? A person in this wretched generation merits to bring people back to repentance; they begin to keep the commandments. His rank is greater than Isaiah’s, Ezekiel’s—than the prophets. Ezekiel revived the dead of the tribe of Ephraim; thousands rose to life. It is written in Duties of the Heart, a thousand years ago. This is one of the great early authorities; it is not some rabbi from a kibbutz today who invented it.

These are the words of our Sages: his rank is greater than the rank of the prophets. So we know that if we go through all the hardship, flights and travel, battles with people, insults from wicked people, and people cursing us to die every ten minutes—look at the comments, every hater of religion—we know that in the end it will pay off enormously; not perhaps, but a billion percent. But someone who does not believe in the World to Come or in reward from heaven—what motivates him to run around hunting observant people and turning them secular? What for? You might say that he gets a great deal in donations, so perhaps he skims a little for his own pocket. But before he began getting donations, he went and founded this organization. He calls it “Hozrim Bitvunah” (“Returning with Understanding”). With them everything is upside down. The most foolish person is called wise. Everything is upside down; the most filthy person calls himself proud. Everything is inverted for them.

In short, ladies and gentlemen, we must learn a moral lesson from these wicked people: how much they are prepared to suffer. He was beaten. I saw a video in which someone hit him. He had opened a booth in the north; some thug came, gave him a few punches, broke up his face, and he began crying like a baby: “It is our fault; we give in to the ultra-Orthodox.” Yet he did not stop. He kept going and setting up booths, knowing that perhaps they would beat him up again. For what? For sheer hatred of the Holy One, blessed be He, and the Torah. There is nothing else. He fears that there will be an observant state here, and for that he sacrifices himself.

We work for the Holy One, blessed be He, and He gives us infinite reward. David writes in Psalms that one reason he kept the commandments and was pious—everything beyond the letter of the law—was “for the reward You have hidden for those who fear You,” for the good You have hidden away. In other words, one reason is that I know I am not working for nothing. You have hidden my reward for the World to Come.

As written at the end of the portion of Va’etchanan, if someone asks how you know there is a World to Come: it is a verse, explicit verses. What does it say? God repays those who love Him and keep His commandments for a thousand generations. Listen; I will read it word for word: “Know therefore that the Lord your God, He is God, the faithful God, who keeps the covenant and kindness to those who love Him and keep His commandments for a thousand generations.” What is a thousand generations? People live one generation. What does a thousand generations mean? Do not worry: there are many generations yet. The reward is waiting for you. You are going to receive a great, great deal for it.

And what is the continuation? “He repays those who hate Him to their face, to destroy them; He will not delay with one who hates Him; to his face He will repay him.” When it comes to Shabbat desecrators, haters of religion, heretics, and all kinds of abominations from the parades here, they receive their reward in this world because they have no World to Come. Rashi writes it explicitly here: “During his lifetime He repays him for his good deed, in order to destroy him from the World to Come.” It is clear. Even without Rashi we understand it; it is very simple.

The question is: why are these wicked people paid anything at all? For what? Their whole lives are hatred of God, hatred of rabbis, hatred of Torah, hatred of everything. So what are they paid for? The answer is that even Yair Lapid performed a circumcision for his son. Tommy Lapid, who used to blaspheme and curse the Holy One, blessed be He, on live television—I personally saw and heard it—performed a circumcision for his son, and perhaps he even had a mezuzah at home. True, they commit endless transgressions, but there is no one who performs no commandments at all.

Even a secular person who comes to a bar mitzvah at a synagogue on Shabbat and is given the opening of the Ark or the taking out of the Torah scroll—this is already a commandment. He circumcised his son: a commandment. He has several mezuzot on his doors: a commandment. At his bar mitzvah the photographer brought tefillin for the pictures: “Put this on for a moment; we will photograph you.” So once in his life he put on tefillin. Perhaps some Chabadnik caught him at an airport and would not leave him alone until he put them on; that too is a commandment. Gather their whole lives together. The Talmud says, “The sinners of Israel are full of commandments like a pomegranate.” Some claim that they counted and a pomegranate has 613 seeds. I do not know; I cannot promise that. But even if it does, that is over an entire lifetime: one commandment here, two there, another here—several hundred commandments over a whole life.

Now the reward for a commandment in the World to Come is infinite. The reward for one commandment is infinite, as we find with Nebuchadnezzar, who wrote a letter. This is what happened: the prophet Isaiah came to King Hezekiah and said, “The Holy One, blessed be He, informed me that you are going to die.” Hezekiah was a great righteous king, who lived 2,600 years ago, one of the greatest kings, at the top of the pyramid together with King David.

He said, “Why must I die?” In his time, every child by age six knew the entire Torah. It is written that he planted a sword in the study hall and said, “Anyone who does not study Torah will be pierced by the sword. I do not play games. Anyone wandering around and playing in the streets rather than being in the study hall will be pierced by the sword.” From fear, everyone became Torah scholars. In the end they came and said to him, “Prepare yourself: you are about to die young.” Why? Because he did not marry. He had to marry and bring children into the world.

He said, “What is revealed and known to me is that I do not marry because I see through divine inspiration that a wicked son will come from me. I do not want to bring such a son into the world. Shall I bring someone into the world who will rebel against God? I decree upon myself to suffer all my life without a wife.” It is written, “It is not good for man to be alone; I will make him a helpmate opposite him,” and he knew that. So he said, “I will suffer all my life and not bring this wicked man into the world.”

The prophet told him: “You cannot make the calculations of the Holy One, blessed be He. You must fulfill the first commandment in the Torah: be fruitful and multiply. What will be, will be. Pray; perhaps things will turn for the good.” What he did not see was that at the end of his days that wicked son would repent. He did not see that; he saw only that he would have a wicked son who would cause the public to sin. And indeed he caused the public to sin for decades, with idols throughout the land. He nearly reached the level of wickedness of Ahab—he was that wicked.

But in the end, the prophet came to him and said, “Prepare yourself; you are going to die.” He said, “I will pray to the Holy One, blessed be He, for forgiveness, I will marry and correct my mistake, and you too should pray. If God agrees, give me your daughter as a wife.” And so it was. The prophet returned and told him, “The Holy One, blessed be He, has added fifteen years to your life.” He was afraid that perhaps the prophet was merely comforting him, so he asked, “By what sign? How will I know?” The prophet said, “Today the sun will not set. If sunset should be at seven, at ten there will still be light in the sky.”

This was one of the few times that the Holy One, blessed be He, froze the sun, the moon, and the earth, as in the time of Joshua son of Nun: “Sun, stand still at Gibeon, and moon, in the Valley of Aijalon.” It remained that way for three days. All the galaxies stood still. At the word of a righteous prophet, all the galaxies froze.

Then, in Iraq—Babylon—they saw that the sun did not set. “The sun has not set!” Everyone was tense: “What is happening? Look, two hours have passed and the sun still has not gone down; something happened in the world.” King Nebuchadnezzar said, “Only the Jews can answer this.” He sent a horseman with a letter: “To the honor of King Hezekiah, the people of the city of Jerusalem, the people of the Land of Israel, and their God: please explain why on such-and-such a day the sun did not set.”

Just as the messenger was about to leave, Nebuchadnezzar made a calculation: “I put their God at the end of the sentence. I should have begun with Him, and afterward mentioned the human king. That is not respectful.” He called the messenger back, stood up from his chair, took three steps—one, two, three—took the letter, and corrected it: “To the honor of the Creator of the world, the Holy One, blessed be He, of the Jews; to the honor of King Hezekiah; to the honor of the people of Jerusalem and the people of the Land of Israel: explain such-and-such.”

What happened? How many steps did he take? Three steps. The Sages say that for the three steps of one wicked man—no less wicked than Hitler; they killed twenty million people—Nebuzaradan, his general, and his army killed twenty million people. Not in a generation like today, where every bomb kills two hundred people, or chemical weapons like those in Syria killed half a million people in a few weeks by gassing them. There were no such weapons. There were swords, spears, and bows. How did they kill? To kill twenty million people—do you know what a task that is? By hand. Think about it: by hand, to kill twenty million people. They murdered twenty million people with their hands and their army. They conquered the entire world; the whole world paid them taxes.

The Sages say that for those three steps he took, the Holy One, blessed be He, gave him dominion over the entire earth for seventy years. He was king over the whole world for forty-five years. His son ruled the entire world for twenty-three years, and his grandson for two years. Altogether they ruled for seventy years. Everyone paid them tribute: the Babylonian empire. Do you hear?

So if this vile man, who rebelled against God, destroyed the Temple built by King Solomon, and murdered countless people—until today the earth is soaked from all they killed—received seventy years of rule because he honored the Holy One, blessed be He, by taking three steps, then what about us? On Shabbat, in this heat in Israel—forty degrees and ninety percent humidity—a person in Bat Yam, Holon, Tel Aviv, or Haifa walks twenty minutes to synagogue. That is thousands of steps. For three steps, this Nazi ruled the entire globe; and we are not Nazis, not murderers. We walk on Shabbat for twenty minutes there and twenty minutes back, three times—for evening, morning, and afternoon prayers: ten thousand steps, twenty thousand steps. It is not for nothing that it says there is a reward for steps.

If for every three steps we received seventy years of rule over the earth, then for one time that you walk to synagogue on Shabbat you deserve seventy thousand years of rule over the earth. But the whole world is allotted only six thousand years. This is proof that the reward for commandments is in the World to Come, as the Talmud in Kiddushin says: the reward for commandments is not in this world. Everything waits for the World to Come.

But the wicked are paid in this world, because in any case they do not believe in the World to Come. They are not prepared to do anything for it. They are satisfied with this world, and only this world interests them. So in the currency you demand, you are paid. If you arrive in New York and want to give someone shekels, he will tell you, “What is this money? This is America, sir—go back and bring dollars. That is the currency here.” In heaven it is a different world, but they want their reward in this world.

Now notice, ladies and gentlemen: this is why at the end of the Amidah we take three steps backward and say, “He who makes peace in His heights,” and immediately afterward, “May it be Your will that the Temple be rebuilt.” What has the Temple to do with it now? We already prayed for the rebuilding of Jerusalem in the middle of the Amidah. Why add the Temple again at the end? Because we step backward and humble ourselves before God. Why do we take three steps backward? Because Nebuchadnezzar took three steps forward, and because of that he destroyed the Temple. So we take three steps backward opposite the three he took forward, and then ask that the Temple be rebuilt. Do you understand? It is as if we are turning the wheel back. Those three steps when he rose from his chair cost us dearly.

This is only to show what the reward for a commandment is. Here it is written that the Holy One, blessed be He, stores the righteous people’s reward for them in the World to Come, but pays the wicked in this world. “He repays those who hate Him”—but for what? For their few commandments: the circumcision, the mezuzot, the one time in a lifetime he put on tefillin, some occasion when he made peace between friends or between a man and his wife, or a guest had nowhere to sleep and arrived in America: “Tell me, can I stay with you for three days?” He welcomed him, fed him, sent him to the airport with sandwiches. That is a Torah commandment. In the end, you will find several thousand commandments that even the greatest haters of Torah have done a little of; that is what they are paid for.

The chief of staff, the defense minister, the prime minister, a Supreme Court justice—opposite those who bring merit to the public there are those who cause the public to sin. One who causes the public to sin is a league of his own. It is written, “One who causes the public to sin is not given the opportunity to repent.” What does that mean? Does it mean he will not merit repentance, that heaven will not let him repent, that God will not accept his repentance? There are many ways to phrase it.

The answer is that the commandments he now keeps are like nothing compared with the sins he causes. He caused a thousand people to sin, and they became wicked because of him. Now he repents and every day performs commandments: tefillin, tallit, grace after meals, prayer—twenty commandments a day, fifty a day; if he is a yeshiva student, perhaps thousands. Fine. But meanwhile he has a thousand wicked students, and everything they learned from him means that they continue in their transgressions. He created a thousand positives and receives ten thousand negatives. He is going backward; there is no progress.

Therefore the Sages tell you: in the natural course of things, even if one day you repent, if you were a great corrupter of the public, there is naturally no way for the scale to turn positive, because the people you caused to sin continue doing evil in the world and it all goes on your account. But because the Holy One, blessed be He, is merciful, loves penitents very much, and does not want to shut the door on them, even in the case of someone like Manasseh, who caused the public to sin for decades—the Talmud said he did not deserve the World to Come. Then the Sages said: if so, You have shut the door on penitents. People will say, “I am already a lost case. I was a DJ in a nightclub for years; boys and girls came, smoking on Shabbat, exchanging phone numbers, committing sins punishable by karet. Who knows how many abortions came from it? It is terrible.” One day you return to repentance and become righteous—but what about all those who went off the proper path at sixteen or seventeen because of you?

Why did I remember this? One of my students is sitting here. He was at our yeshiva in New York, he and his brother; I met both of them in Los Angeles. In Los Angeles there is a synagogue that used to be a magnificent church—three thousand seats. Persian Jews from Beverly Hills bought it, renovated the entire place, painted and replaced everything, brought in chairs from some kibbutz here in Israel, and made a magnificent enormous synagogue.

One day I went to speak there on Shabbat. After the sermon, those two brothers came to me. They lived in Los Angeles with their family. In perhaps forty minutes of conversation—no more than that—I explained to them how grave it is to be a DJ and cause the public to sin at parties. Ladies and gentlemen, this is unprecedented: without hesitation they immediately gave up all the honor, money, fame, and admiration of the fans, the music they played. They stopped.

No wonder that the Holy One, blessed be He, immediately granted them the merit to come to yeshiva, afterward to come to Israel, marry, become kollel scholars, and establish magnificent homes. Now one can be a DJ for a kosher wedding: you earn money and it is also a commandment. You make people happy at their wedding and earn nearly the same money.

I had a similar case. One day they told me, “Listen, there is the most famous DJ in the history of New York—a man of Israeli background, but he is American; his parents are of Israeli origin.”
