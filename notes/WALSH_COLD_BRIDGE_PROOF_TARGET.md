# Walsh recovery: the cold syndrome-bridge proof target

Date: 2026-08-13.

This note corrects and sharpens the harmonic address-preserving combiner target
in `notes/WALSH_TRANSPORT_PROOF_TARGET.md`.

## 1. Separate cooling from address restoration

Take two independent wide samples that collide modulo `2L*` and write

    Y = (X_1+X_2)/2,    D = (X_1-X_2)/2.

The Gaussian parallelogram identity makes `Y` and `D` cold at width parameter
`r=R/2`, subject to their parity constraints.  Marginalizing the difference
variable gives cold samples on `L*`, but removes the `h` fixed affine parity
bits.  The recovery problem can therefore be split into two operations:

1. exact Gaussian combination from the wide half-coset to `D_(L*,xi_r)`;
2. cold syndrome folding from `D_(L*,xi_r)` to `D_(Lambda_j,xi_r)`.

Only the second operation is missing.  It need not change temperature.

## 2. Exact Fourier conservation at the combiner

Split every parity address into `h` head bits and `ell=n-h` tail bits.  Let
`q=V_P(Y)`, `d=V_P(D)`, and let `b=q_tail+d_tail` be the retained collision
bucket.  The original affine constraint becomes

    q_head+d_head=j.

For a scalar or matrix observable `F(q)` and difference mass `G(d)`, define
the mixed transform

    T(a,c) = sum_(q,d: q_head+d_head=j)
             F(q) G(d) (-1)^(a.q+c.b).

Expanding the head constraint with its Walsh delta gives the exact identity

    T(a,c)
      = 2^(-h) sum_(lambda in F_2^h) (-1)^(lambda.j)
          Fhat(a_head+lambda, a_tail+c)
          Ghat(lambda,c).

This is checked exhaustively in
`tests/walsh_combiner_fourier_obstruction_test.py`.

The linear target coefficient `Fhat(alpha*,theta*)` can be multiplied by the
trivial difference coefficient `Ghat(0,0)` only by taking

    lambda=0, c=0, a=(alpha*,theta*).

That is the full `n`-bit query.  If the algorithm queries only the tail and
fixes `a_head=0`, then `lambda=alpha*`; the target is multiplied by the
nontrivial difference coefficient `Ghat(alpha*,c)`.  This is the previously
observed signal squaring.  It is a Fourier-conservation law, not an artifact
of the chosen estimator.

## 3. Why an oblivious harmonic fiber does not pay for the prefix

Let `N=2^h` and let the missing prefix `alpha*` be uniform, as it is after the
random `GL_n(F_2)` change of coordinates.  For any target-independent rank-`d`
orthogonal sketch `S:R^N -> R^d`,

    E_alpha ||S e_alpha||^2 = ||S||_F^2/N = d/N.

Amplifying by `sqrt(N/d)` to restore constant target energy multiplies the
noise variance by `N/d`.  Thus an oblivious moving harmonic subspace does not
create an exponential SNR credit in this estimation problem.  The code-bound
projection theorem gains from a negative off-diagonal separation inequality;
the Walsh estimator has no corresponding sign constraint.

The harmonic idea can still help only if its fiber is state-dependent and
implements an actual transport or conditional bridge.

## 4. The weakest surviving transport theorem

Put

    pi = D_(L*,xi_r),
    A_j = Lambda_j = {x in L*: J_P(x)=j},
    pi_j = pi(. | A_j).

The exact combiner supplies samples from `pi`.  It is enough to prove:

> **Cold syndrome-folding theorem.**  For random `P` and `j`, after
> `2^(n/2+o(n))` preprocessing there is a Markov kernel `K_j` from `L*` to
> `A_j`, samplable in `2^o(n)` amortized time, such that for `q=pi K_j`,
>
>     1 + chi^2(pi_j || q) <= 2^((delta+o(1))n)
>
> with `delta < 1/2-2r`.

At the exact-combiner width

    r = 0.23675858...,
    1/2-2r = 0.02648284... .

Then `2^((2r+delta+o(1))n)` folded samples suffice for Hessian concentration,
while both exact combination and the remaining `ell=n/2` Walsh transform cost
`2^(n/2+o(n))`.

This theorem is strictly weaker than the earlier wide-to-cold transport
target: its input already has the correct cold radial law.

## 5. A sufficient Doob-bridge lemma

Let `P` be a `pi`-reversible Markov kernel on `L*`.  For a horizon `T`, define
the backward committor

    h_k(x) = Pr_x[X_(T-k) in A_j],    0 <= k <= T.

It obeys the backward heat equation

    h_k = P h_(k+1),    h_T = 1_(A_j).

Where `h_k(x)>0`, the time-inhomogeneous Doob transition is

    P_k^j(x,y) = P(x,y) h_(k+1)(y)/h_k(x).

Every path generated by these transitions ends in `A_j`.  Start the bridge
from `X_0~pi`, and let `q_T` be its endpoint law.  If

    2^(-epsilon n) pi(A_j)
      <= h_0(x)
      <= 2^(epsilon n) pi(A_j)

uniformly on the starting support, then reversibility gives, for `z in A_j`,

    q_T(z)/pi_j(z)
      = pi(A_j) E_[X~P^T(z,.)][1/h_0(X)]
      in [2^(-epsilon n),2^(epsilon n)].

Consequently,

    1+chi^2(pi_j || q_T) <= 2^(epsilon n).

Therefore the cold syndrome-folding theorem follows with `delta=epsilon` if
we construct a reversible chain satisfying all of:

1. **relative syndrome mixing:** the committor bound holds with
   `epsilon<0.02648284...`;
2. **compressed committors:** `h_k(x)` is computable from a state descriptor
   of at most `n/2+o(n)` bits, ideally only `J_P(x)` plus polynomially many
   radial/energy levels;
3. **samplable Doob steps:** the tilted neighbor distribution can be sampled
   in `2^o(n)` time;
4. **short horizon:** `T=2^o(n)`.

The logarithmic potential `S_k=-log h_k` satisfies a discrete viscous
Hamilton--Jacobi equation.  In the small-noise limit its leading equation is
the eikonal equation.  This is the precise role for the eikonal machinery:
compute or approximate the committor that conditions a cold stationary walk
onto the lost parity syndrome without rejection.

## 6. What simple folding does not prove

`experiments/walsh_syndrome_folding_transport.py` maps each input syndrome to
`j` by its shortest enumerated translation.  In dimensions 4--6 its worst
Renyi action is `0.46` to `0.83` bits per dimension, versus the required
`0.02648`.  Even the best observed coset is generally above budget.  Fixed
syndrome translations therefore reproduce the same large-deviation barrier
as local contraction.

The Doob kernel must be state-dependent: it must route different points in
one syndrome through different corrections according to their cold Gaussian
action.  Equivalently, the proof now needs a low-complexity approximation to
the committor, not merely a table of one representative per syndrome.

## 7. Exact finite Doob audit

`experiments/walsh_doob_syndrome_bridge.py` builds the lazy nearest-neighbor
Metropolis chain for the cold Gaussian on a truncated generic lattice,
computes the full backward committor, and evaluates the endpoint law of the
Doob bridge without Monte Carlo error.  Detailed balance and the bridge
Renyi bound are checked algebraically.

For dimensions 3 and 4, cutoff 2, and horizons 4 through 128, the worst
observed endpoint action is already below budget at the first horizon:

    n=3, T=4:  0.000450 bits/dimension,
    n=4, T=4:  0.004026 bits/dimension,
    required:  0.026483 bits/dimension.

The action then falls rapidly toward numerical zero.  Fixed translations on
the same kind of instances fail badly, so the improvement comes from the
state-dependent committor tilt rather than ordinary local contraction.

This finite result does not prove a general-lattice mixing statement.  It
does show that the Renyi target itself is plausible and identifies the sole
algorithmic obstruction: the audit stores `h_k(x)` on every lattice point,
whereas the theorem permits only `2^(n/2+o(n))` preprocessing and subexponential
work per sample.  We therefore need to prove that the relevant committor can
be represented or approximated by

    h_k(x) approximately H_k(J_P(x), E_1(x), ..., E_m(x))

with `m=poly(n)` or `2^o(n)` energy/eikonal state variables, while preserving
the endpoint Renyi bound.  This **compressed-committor lemma** is now the
single proof target.

## 8. Approximate compressed-committor theorem

The probabilistic part can be proved with approximate potentials; exact
committors are unnecessary.

Let `P` be `pi`-reversible, let `A=A_j`, and put

    h_k = P^(T-k) 1_A.

Choose nonnegative approximations `htilde_0,...,htilde_T`, with
`htilde_T=1_A`, and define normalized tilted transitions

    Ptilde_k(x,y)
      = P(x,y) htilde_(k+1)(y)
        / (P htilde_(k+1))(x).

Assume all denominators on the starting support are positive.  Define the
backward-heat residuals

    rho_k(x) = (P htilde_(k+1))(x)/htilde_k(x).

Suppose, in base-two logarithms,

    |log2(h_0(x)/pi(A))| <= epsilon_0 n,
    |log2(h_0(x)/htilde_0(x))| <= epsilon_1 n,
    |log2 rho_k(x)| <= eta_k n.

Let `qtilde_T` be the endpoint law obtained by starting from `pi` and using
the transitions `Ptilde_k`.  Then

    1 + chi^2(pi(.|A) || qtilde_T)
      <= 2^((epsilon_0+epsilon_1+sum_k eta_k)n).       (B)

### Proof

Let `Q` be the exact Doob path law and `Qtilde` the approximate path law.
For a path `x_0,...,x_T`, telescoping the approximate potentials gives

    Qtilde(path)
      = pi(x_0) [prod_k P(x_k,x_(k+1))] 1_A(x_T)
        / htilde_0(x_0) prod_k rho_k(x_k)^(-1).

The exact path density is the same expression with denominator `h_0(x_0)`
and no residual product.  Therefore

    dQtilde/dQ
      = h_0(x_0)/htilde_0(x_0)
        prod_k rho_k(x_k)^(-1)

lies between `2^(-E n)` and `2^(E n)`, where

    E = epsilon_1 + sum_k eta_k.

Pointwise likelihood-ratio bounds survive marginalization, so the same bound
holds between the two endpoint laws.

For the exact bridge, reversibility gives, for `z in A`,

    q_T(z)/pi(z|A)
      = pi(A) E_[X~P^T(z,.)][1/h_0(X)].

The `epsilon_0` assumption puts this ratio in
`[2^(-epsilon_0 n),2^(epsilon_0 n)]`.  Multiplying the two endpoint-ratio
bounds yields

    pi(z|A)/qtilde_T(z) <= 2^((E+epsilon_0)n).

Averaging this ratio under `pi(.|A)` proves (B).  QED.

The finite-dimensional identity and inequality are independently checked in
`tests/walsh_approximate_doob_certificate_test.py`.

### Algorithmic corollary

Suppose every `htilde_k(x)` is evaluable from a descriptor with at most
`n/2+o(n)` bits of tabulated state, every tilted step has `2^o(n)` candidate
neighbors, `T=2^o(n)`, and

    epsilon_0 + epsilon_1 + sum_k eta_k
      < 1/2-2r = 0.02648284... .

Then the approximate bridge proves the cold syndrome-folding theorem and,
combined with the existing Hessian recovery, gives the `2^(n/2+o(n))` SVP
algorithm.

This completes the bridge proof.  What remains unproved is the geometric
existence statement: constructing such compressed potentials and a samplable
base chain uniformly for arbitrary lattices.

## 9. Why the current lattice ingredients do not prove existence

Three possible closures can now be rejected precisely.

### Stronger exact collision

The ordinary exact combine matches only the `ell=n-h` free parity bits.  With
`h=ell=n/2` and `N=2^(n/2)` wide samples, the bucket load is constant and it
produces `Theta(N)` cold full-lattice outputs.  Preserving the affine prefix
requires matching modulo `2 Lambda_0`, an `n`-bit key.  Above smoothing these
keys are asymptotically uniform, so the expected number of pairs is only

    N^2/2^n = Theta(1),

not the required `2^(2rn)` batch.  The stronger collision proves the right
distribution but not the required throughput.

### Progressive index-two conditioning

At the exact-combiner target `r=0.23675858...`, the improved spherical-count
audit gives

    g_2(r) = 0.02058146... .

The existing smoothing argument can therefore impose only about `0.0206n`
random affine constraints at that width.  Iterating it cannot reach the
required `h=n/2`; after that point the sublattice sampler's smoothing
hypothesis is false.

### Generic local Metropolis mixing

The finite local chain mixes well on the tested generic lattices, but no
basis-independent spectral-gap theorem at this parameter is available.  A
bad lattice basis can make coordinate proposals exponentially unlikely, and
geometric ergodicity alone supplies no exponent below `0.02648`.  Existing
Metropolis--Klein rates depend on basis quality or theta-series minorization,
which is exactly the missing geometric estimate.

Therefore (B) is a proved reduction theorem, but the unconditional
`2^(n/2+o(n))` SVP conclusion is not proved.  Claiming otherwise would assume
the new compressed-committor/spectral-gap result that the project is trying
to discover.

## 10. Stationary seeding removes the bridge

There is a better initialization than conditioning a full-lattice walk on a
preselected rare syndrome.  Draw

    X_0 ~ D_(Lambda,s),        P ~ uniform GL_n(F_2),

independently, and only then set `j=J_P(X_0)`.  Conditional on fixed `P,j`,
the seed has exactly the desired law

    Law(X_0 | P,j) = D_(Lambda_j,s).                       (C)

Thus any `D_(Lambda_j,s)`-stationary kernel starts in stationarity.  There is
no burn-in, committor, or rare-event entrance action.  The only issue is that
the resulting `(P,j)` must be close to the independent uniform choice used by
the Hessian recovery theorem.  The following exact identity proves this.

### Parity collision lemma

Let `U=X mod 2 Lambda` for `X~D_(Lambda,s)`.  Then

    Col(U) := sum_u Pr[U=u]^2
            = (rho_(s/sqrt(2))(Lambda)/rho_s(Lambda))^2.  (D)

Indeed, the numerator of the collision probability is the Gaussian mass of
pairs `x,y in Lambda` with `x=y mod 2 Lambda`.  The bijection

    z=(x+y)/2,  w=(x-y)/2

maps those pairs to `Lambda x Lambda`, and the parallelogram identity gives

    rho_s(x) rho_s(y)
      = rho_(s/sqrt(2))(z) rho_(s/sqrt(2))(w).

If `s>sqrt(2) eta_(1/2)(Lambda)`, Poisson summation gives

    rho_(s/sqrt(2))(Lambda)/rho_s(Lambda)
      = 2^(-n/2)
        rho_(sqrt(2)/s)(Lambda*)/rho_(1/s)(Lambda*)
      <= (3/2) 2^(-n/2),

and hence

    Col(U) <= (9/4) 2^(-n).                               (E)

### Full-rank leftover-hash lemma

Let `H:F_2^n -> F_2^h` be uniform among surjective linear maps.  For every
nonzero `v`,

    Pr_H[H(v)=0] = (2^(n-h)-1)/(2^n-1) =: q_(n,h).

For independent copies `U,U'`, split according to `U=U'`.  This gives the
exact formula

    E_H[chi^2(Law(HU)) || Uniform_h]
      = 2^h [Col(U)+(1-Col(U))q_(n,h)]-1.                 (F)

Combining (E) and (F), for `h=n/2`,

    E_H chi^2 = O(2^(-n/2)),
    E_H TV(Law(HU),Uniform_h) = O(2^(-n/4)).             (G)

The first `h` rows of uniform `P in GL_n(F_2)` are precisely a uniform
surjective `H`.  Equations (C) and (G) therefore show that the joint law of

    (P, j, X_0)

is exponentially close to: choose independent uniform `P,j`, then draw
`X_0~D_(Lambda_j,s)`.  At `s=xi_r`, the hypothesis follows from `r>t_0` and
Corollary 3.2/Theorem 2.7 of the source paper.

The finite theta-series and full-rank-hash identities are checked in
`experiments/walsh_stationary_seed_reduction.py` and
`tests/walsh_stationary_seed_reduction_test.py`.

## 11. The geometric clause is now exactly a gap theorem

For fixed good `P,j`, let `pi_j=D_(Lambda_j,xi_r)` and let `K_(P,j)` be a
lazy reversible transition kernel on `Lambda_j`.  Suppose:

1. one transition is samplable in `2^o(n)` amortized time;
2. its absolute spectral gap satisfies

       gamma_(P,j) >= 2^(-(delta+o(1))n);                (H)

3. (H) fails with only exponentially small probability for the seed-derived
   `(P,j)`.

Start the kernel at the stationary seed from Section 10.  For every centered
square-integrable scalar observable `f`, reversibility gives

    |Cov(f(X_0),f(X_k))|
      <= (1-gamma)^k Var_pi_j(f).

Consequently

    Var[(1/M) sum_(i=0)^(M-1) f(X_i)]
      <= 2 Var_pi_j(f)/(M gamma).                         (I)

The same spectral calculation, or the standard spectral-gap Bernstein
inequality for bounded reversible chains, applies entrywise to the truncated
Walsh-Hessian summands.  Hence Hhan's independent-sample proof goes through
with effective sample size `M gamma`.  Taking

    M = 2^((2r+delta+o(1))n)

and performing the length-`2^ell` Walsh transform gives total exponent

    max{1/2, 2r+delta}.

It is therefore sufficient to prove

    delta < 1/2-2r = 0.02648284... .                     (J)

This is strictly stronger and cleaner than the earlier Doob clause: the
committor flatness, approximation residual, and endpoint divergence have all
disappeared.  The stationary-seed lemma proves the geometric *entrance*
clause.  The only unproved statement is now (H)-(J), a basis-independent gap
bound for an explicitly samplable coset-preserving kernel.

## 12. Immediate constraint on the kernel

A temperature-only ladder does not prove (H).  If a configuration is merely
moved between adjacent temperatures and refreshed only at the hot endpoint,
a typical cold point has total likelihood ratio

    pi_hot(x)/pi_cold(x) = 2^(-Theta(n)).

Fine adjacent overlap controls each accepted move but does not remove the
aggregate drift back toward the cold endpoint; reaching the refresh level can
still be exponentially unlikely.  A successful kernel must therefore mutate
the lattice point at intermediate scales, or directly use nonlocal
syndrome-zero moves.  This rules out treating replica exchange by itself as
the missing existence proof.

## 13. A scalar geometric inequality that would finish the proof

The independent Metropolis--Klein kernel turns (H) into an explicit theta
quotient.  Write the affine target as

    Lambda_j = x_j + Lambda_0

and let `C=(c_1,...,c_n)` be any basis of `Lambda_0`, with Gram--Schmidt
vectors `ctilde_i`.  Klein's backward sampler is an independent proposal on
the coefficient lattice.  In the `rho_s(x)=exp(-pi||x||^2/s^2)` convention,
the independent-MH minorization, and hence the lazy-chain gap up to a factor
two, is

    Delta(C,x_j,s)
      = rho_(s,-x_j)(Lambda_0)
        / prod_i rho_(s/||ctilde_i||)(Z).                (K)

This is the spectral-gap formula proved for independent MHK; the shift is in
the numerator, while replacing the one-dimensional shifted theta masses by
their central maxima gives the displayed uniform lower bound.  A proposal and
accept/reject step cost polynomial time once `C` is given.

Consequently the unconditional recovery theorem is reduced to the following
single geometric statement.

> **Random-half-kernel Klein-defect theorem (proof target).**  For
> `Lambda=L*`, `s=xi_r`, uniform `P`, and the seed-derived `j`, one can find a
> basis `C_P` of `Lambda_0=ker J_P` in `2^(n/2+o(n))` preprocessing time such
> that, except with exponentially small probability,
>
>     log2 [prod_i rho_(s/||ctilde_(P,i)||)(Z)
>           / rho_(s,-x_j)(Lambda_0)]
>       <= (delta+o(1))n
>
> for some `delta<0.02648284...`.

If this theorem holds, (K) implies (H), the stationary-seed argument supplies
the exact initial law, (I) supplies the effective samples, and (J) gives the
`2^(n/2+o(n))` running time.  No further probabilistic or analytic lemma is
missing.

This formulation also identifies the obstruction in ordinary reduction:
LLL/BKZ controls the individual Gram--Schmidt lengths, but the required bound
is on their *aggregate theta defect relative to the shifted lattice mass*.
A worst-coordinate estimate throws away precisely the `0.02648n` budget.

### Determinant cancellation

The theta quotient in (K) simplifies further for the seed-derived address.
Let

    p_(P,j) = rho_s(Lambda_j)/rho_s(Lambda).

Since `j` is sampled from `p_(P,.)`, for every fixed `alpha>0`, without using
any uniformity estimate,

    Pr[p_(P,j) < 2^(-h-alpha n) | P] <= 2^(-alpha n).    (L)

This is just the sum of at most `2^h` probabilities, each below the displayed
threshold.  Also, since `s=xi_r` with `r>t_0`, Poisson summation and the source
paper's Gaussian-mass bound give

    rho_s(Lambda)
      = (s^n/det Lambda)(1+2^(-Omega(n))).               (M)

Because `det Lambda_0=2^h det Lambda`, (L)-(M) imply, for a
seed-typical address,

    rho_(s,-x_j)(Lambda_0)
      >= 2^(-alpha n-o(n)) s^n/det Lambda_0.             (N)

Now put `g_i=||ctilde_i||`.  The one-dimensional Poisson identity and
`prod_i g_i=det Lambda_0` give the exact cancellation

    prod_i rho_(s/g_i)(Z)
      = (s^n/det Lambda_0) prod_i rho_(g_i/s)(Z).         (O)

Combining (K), (N), and (O),

    -log2 Delta(C,x_j,s)
      <= sum_i log2 rho_(||ctilde_i||/s)(Z)
         + alpha n + o(n).                               (P)

Thus the sharpest form of the missing geometric theorem is:

> With exponentially high probability over the random half-kernel, find a
> basis `C_P` in `2^(n/2+o(n))` preprocessing time satisfying
>
>     sum_i log2 rho_(||ctilde_(P,i)||/xi_r)(Z)
>       < (0.02648284-o(1))n.                            (Q)

The arbitrary `alpha>0` in (L) can be absorbed in the strict inequality.
Equation (Q) is the exact orthogonalization tax allowed by the recovery
budget.  It is now the sole geometric existence clause: proving (Q) proves
the theorem; a counterexample to (Q) for every efficiently obtainable basis
would rule out Metropolis--Klein as the closing kernel without affecting the
stationary-seed reduction.

For scale, the one-dimensional equation

    log2 rho_tau(Z) = 0.02648284...

has `tau=0.81916596...`.  Therefore the stronger but very transparent
condition

    max_i ||ctilde_(P,i)|| <= 0.81916 xi_r

would prove (Q).  It is not known from ordinary LLL/BKZ or smoothing bounds;
the aggregate form (Q), which permits a small number of longer
Gram--Schmidt vectors compensated by shorter ones, is the more plausible
target.

### The factor-sqrt(2) near miss

There is real extra room at the MHK scale.  The DGS smoothing test for the
cold width `xi_r` examines

    rho_(sqrt(2)/xi_r)(Lambda_0* \ {0}),

and the improved spherical calculation gives only

    g_2(r)=0.02058... < h/n=1/2.

The determinant cancellation in (O), by contrast, involves

    rho_(1/xi_r)(Lambda_0* \ {0}).

Since `1/xi_r=sqrt(2)/xi_(2r)`, the same calculation is evaluated at `2r`
and gives

    g_2(2r)=0.52040... > 1/2.                         (R)

Thus a random half-kernel is exponentially flat at exactly the dual scale
appearing in (O), even though it is not smooth enough for the existing DGS
refresh theorem.  This proves (M)-(N) with exponential slack and explains why
MHK is the right candidate family.

It does **not** prove (Q).  The inequality valid for every fixed basis is

    rho_(1/s)(Lambda_0*)
      <= prod_i rho_(||ctilde_i||/s)(Z),

in the wrong direction: centralizing every one-dimensional conditional in
Klein's triangular proposal can create an exponential orthogonalization tax
even when the full dual theta mass is `1+2^(-Omega(n))`.  Therefore (R) cannot
be substituted for (Q).  A proof still needs either a basis-selection theorem
controlling that tax below `0.02648284n`, or a non-triangular kernel whose
minorization is controlled directly by the full dual theta mass.

## 14. The flatness-controlled Ornstein--Uhlenbeck kernel

There is a canonical non-triangular candidate.  Let `A=x_j+Lambda_0`, fix
`0<=a<1`, and put

    tau=s sqrt(1-a^2).

From a state `x in A`, propose

    q_x(y) = rho_tau(y-a x)/rho_tau(A-a x),   y in A.    (S)

The Gaussian quadratic identity

    ||x||^2/s^2 + ||y-a x||^2/tau^2
      = ||y||^2/s^2 + ||x-a y||^2/tau^2               (T)

shows that the Metropolis ratio for target `D_(A,s)` is exactly

    [pi(y)q_y(x)]/[pi(x)q_x(y)]
      = rho_tau(A-a x)/rho_tau(A-a y).                  (U)

There is no basis or Gram--Schmidt term.  Poisson summation gives, uniformly
for every real shift `t`,

    |rho_tau(Lambda_0+t)/(tau^n/det Lambda_0)-1|
      <= epsilon_tau,
    epsilon_tau=rho_(1/tau)(Lambda_0* \ {0}).           (V)

Consequently every proposal is accepted with probability at least

    (1-epsilon_tau)/(1+epsilon_tau).                    (W)

This is the desired use of full dual flatness.  The exact identities,
reversibility, and finite gaps are audited in
`experiments/walsh_flatness_ou_kernel.py`.

The kernel is not yet an algorithm.  Its proposal is shifted DGS at width

    tau=s sqrt(1-a^2) < s.

At the random-half-kernel cold scale we know flatness at `s`, but the existing
ADRS batch sampler requires its output width to exceed
`sqrt(2) eta_(1/2)(Lambda_0)`.  Indeed the spherical calculation says the
dual mass is small at `1/s` but exponentially large at `sqrt(2)/s`.  Therefore
no nonzero `a` makes (S) accessible to that sampler.  The OU construction
proves that shifted-theta normalization is not the obstacle; generating the
conditional proposal is.

## 15. Why random-superlattice refresh does not implement it

Kim's dual-sparsification sampler makes a superlattice smooth at a parameter
`t` and samples it at `sqrt(2)t`.  To obtain cold width `s`, it must take
`t=s/sqrt(2)`.  For `Lambda_0` the required superlattice index is governed by

    rho_(sqrt(2)/s)(Lambda_0*)
      = 2^((1/2-g_2(r)+o(1))n)
      = 2^(0.47943...n).

One ADRS call returns `2^(n/2)` superlattice samples, but the sharp Gaussian
mass comparison loses `2^(n/2)`: only an inverse-polynomial number land back
in the target lattice in the worst case.  In our more refined count, the
cold retained population has exponent only

    1/2-(1/2-g_2(r)) = g_2(r)=0.02057...,

versus the required `2r=0.47352...`.  Reusing a fixed superlattice does not
alter this mass ratio, and repeating calls adds their exponents.  Thus simple
superlattice rejection cannot yield a kernel with `delta<0.02648`.

Kim also proves that the `2^(n/2)` Gaussian-mass loss is tight for every
superlattice satisfying the required smoothing condition.  Any successful
reuse must therefore transport the rejected cosets, not merely resample and
filter them.

## 16. Revised viable target

The remaining target is now one of the following equivalent algorithmic
objects:

1. an amortized sampler for the shifted proposals (S), after
   `2^(n/2+o(n))` preprocessing, costing at most
   `2^((0.02648284-o(1))n)` per effective transition;
2. a non-triangular proposal `q` on `A` samplable with that amortized cost and
   satisfying

       sup_x D_(A,s)(x)/q(x)
         <= 2^((0.02648284-o(1))n);                     (X)

3. a transport of all cosets rejected by the random-superlattice sampler into
   `A` whose Renyi-2 action is below `0.02648284n`.

Independent Metropolis with (X) has the required gap immediately.  The OU
calculation shows what the ideal geometry looks like; the superlattice
tightness result shows that rejection alone cannot realize it.  This is a
strictly narrower target than a generic lattice Poincare inequality.

## 17. Progressive deterministic folding is not the clean remaining route

There is a sharper finite version of target 3.  Impose the `h` syndrome bits
one at a time.  At an index-two step, bijectively match every state in the
rejected sibling to a state in the retained child and fold its probability
mass there.  If `p_x` is the current mass on a rejected state and `p_y` the
mass on a retained state, the next proposal mass is `p_y+p_x`.  Hence the
matching that minimizes the next target-to-proposal Renyi-2 action is exactly
the linear assignment with cost

    c(x,y) = pi_next(y)^2/(p_y+p_x).                    (Y)

This proves optimality for one deterministic folding step; for several bits
the greedy assignments need not be globally optimal.  The finite audit in
`experiments/walsh_progressive_coset_transport.py` compares (Y) with minimum
squared-displacement matching.

The corrected audit draws the target prefix from the stationary seed rather
than uniformly.  This matters in low dimension: the observed prefix masses
range from `0.53` to `0.86`, far from the asymptotic leftover-hash regime.  In
dimension 4 the best two-step action is `0.02143` bits/dimension, below the
`0.02648` budget, while the geometric matching costs `0.04936`.  The other
finite cases are above budget.  Therefore this architecture is not ruled out,
but the experiment identifies an additional theorem it would require: an
efficient local approximation to the mass-ranked assignment (Y).  Since the
stationary seed already removes the need to transport into the coset, a direct
stationary kernel is cleaner.

## 18. The random-line heat-bath kernel

There is a direct kernel that avoids every unavailable multidimensional DGS
call.  Let `V={v_1,...,v_m}` be nonzero vectors in `Lambda_0`.  For each `v`,
define the heat-bath projection

    H_v(x,y) = pi_j(y)/pi_j(x+Z v),  if y-x in Z v,
               0,                    otherwise,         (Z)

and take

    K_V = (1/m) sum_(v in V) H_v.                       (AA)

The update is explicitly samplable.  Conditional on the current line,

    pi_j(x+k v)
      proportional to exp[-pi ||v||^2/s^2
                            (k+<x,v>/||v||^2)^2],

so one transition is only a one-dimensional shifted discrete Gaussian
`D_(Z+c,s/||v||)`.  No smoothing hypothesis is needed to sample this
one-dimensional conditional.

The operator facts are exact.  Detailed balance follows because for two
points on the same line

    pi_j(x) H_v(x,y)
      = pi_j(x)pi_j(y)/pi_j(x+Z v)
      = pi_j(y) H_v(y,x).

Moreover `H_v f=E[f(X) | X mod Z v]`; it is an orthogonal conditional-
expectation projection in `L^2(pi_j)`.  Thus it is self-adjoint, idempotent,
and positive semidefinite.  Averaging gives the exact gap identity

    gap(K_V)
      = inf_(Var_pi_j f>0)
          [(1/m) sum_(v in V)
             E_pi_j Var(f(X) | X mod Z v)]/Var_pi_j(f). (AB)

If the integer span of `V` is `Lambda_0`, the chain is irreducible.  Starting
from the seed in Section 10, it is stationary from time zero.

This proves all algorithmic and Markov-chain clauses except one geometric
functional inequality.  A sufficient remaining theorem is:

> **Random-half mobile-frame tensorization theorem.**  In
> `2^(n/2+o(n))` preprocessing time, construct a generating family
> `V subset Lambda_0` of size `2^o(n)` such that, with exponentially high
> probability over the random half-kernel and seed-derived address,
>
>     Var_pi_j(f)
>       <= 2^((delta+o(1))n) (1/|V|) sum_(v in V)
>            E_pi_j Var(f(X) | X mod Z v)               (AC)
>
> for every `f in L^2(pi_j)` and some
> `delta<0.02648284...`.

By (AB), (AC) is exactly the needed gap bound.  Unlike the Klein-defect
condition (Q), it permits a redundant frame rather than charging a product of
`n` triangular theta maxima.

The theorem naturally separates into two more geometric statements.  Define
the linear relaxation number by restricting the quotient in (AB) to
`f_u(x)=<u,x>`.  It is enough to prove

1. a short-vector/frame construction with linear relaxation
   `2^(-o(n))`; and
2. a nonlinear comparison
   `gap(K_V) >= 2^(-o(n)) gap_linear(K_V)`.

The second clause is the discrete, random-line analogue of Gaussian
approximate tensorization.  It is now the proof target; it is substantially
narrower than shifted DGS or arbitrary lattice Poincare.

`experiments/walsh_line_heat_bath_kernel.py` audits the exact finite matrices.
For all enumerated 4-dimensional affine cosets, the raw kernel-generator gaps
are `0.0376`--`0.1421`.  Merely taking the shortest `O(n)` directions can fail
to span; a mobility-weighted leverage selection repairs this.  Its gaps are
`0.0841`--`0.1497`, while the corresponding linear upper bounds are
`0.0881`--`0.1558`.  Thus the nonlinear-to-linear ratios are about
`0.86`--`0.96` in these cases.  Boundary mass is below `1.4e-4`.

These finite spectra do not prove (AC), but they change the live target.  The
remaining existence clause is no longer an `n`-dimensional cold sampler.  It
is a short mobile-frame construction plus a discrete Gaussian tensorization
inequality for exact one-dimensional line projections.

## 19. Necessary mobile-shell threshold

The line construction has a sharp asymptotic guardrail.  It is important not
to confuse the flatness information about `Lambda_0*` with the short
directions required in `Lambda_0`.

Let `X` be stationary and let `Y` be one `H_v` update.  On each line, write
`p_x=pi_j(x)` and `p_line=sum_(z in x+Z v) p_z`.  Then

    Pr[Y != X]
      = sum_(x != y, y-x in Z v) p_x p_y/p_line
      <= (1/2) sum_(k != 0) sum_x sqrt(p_x p_(x+kv)).    (AD)

The Gaussian parallelogram identity gives the exact overlap

    sum_x sqrt(p_x p_(x+kv))
      = exp[-pi k^2||v||^2/(4s^2)]
        rho_s(A+kv/2)/rho_s(A).                          (AE)

At the target scale, (R) and Poisson summation make the shifted-theta ratio
in (AE) `1+2^(-Omega(n))`, uniformly in the shift.  Therefore

    Pr[Y != X]
      <= (1+2^(-Omega(n)))
         sum_(k>=1) exp[-pi k^2||v||^2/(4s^2)].          (AF)

This also upper-bounds the gap up to `1+o(1)` when the stationary collision
probability is negligible.  One way to see this is to assign independent
Rademacher values to the states: the expected Dirichlet form is the move
probability, while the expected variance is `1-Col(pi_j)`, so some assignment
has their ratio no larger than the ratio of those expectations.

If every selected direction has

    ||v|| >= a sqrt(n) s,

then (AF) yields

    gap(K_V) <= 2^(-(pi a^2/(4 ln 2)-o(1))n).           (AG)

Consequently the `0.02648284...` budget requires mobile directions below

    a_critical
      = sqrt(4(0.02648284...) ln 2/pi)
      = 0.15287980... .                                  (AH)

More generally, enough directional leverage must lie below this shell; a few
short collinear vectors do not suffice.  This is the exact geometric test for
the proposed frame constructor.

The known dual flatness at `1/s` does not imply (AH), because it counts vectors
of `Lambda_0*`, not `Lambda_0`.  Thus (AC) remains a genuine theorem target,
not a consequence of Section 14.  If no spanning mobile frame exists at this
radius, the next surviving kernel is a rank-`b` block heat bath: resample on
`x+W` for a low-rank sublattice `W subset Lambda_0`.  Taking `b=o(n)` preserves
subexponential per-transition work, while a block can cross a direction that
is frozen for every one-dimensional update.  The corresponding remaining
target is the block analogue of (AC), with conditional variance given the
quotient by `W`.

## 20. The recovery does not require an absolute gap

The transport work elsewhere in this repository makes a useful distinction:
separate the residual visible to the transported observable from orthogonal
state that the decoder never consumes.  Applied here, (AC) is stronger than
the Walsh recovery theorem needs.

Let `K` be any positive semidefinite reversible kernel on `pi_j`.  For a tail
character `theta` and a symmetric test matrix `B`, center the scalar
Walsh--Hessian observable

    f_(theta,B)(x)
      = <B, xx^T-||x||^2 I/n>_F (-1)^(theta.V_tail(x)).

If `mu_f` is its spectral measure under `K`, define its observable relaxation
factor

    tau(f;K)
      = [integral (1+lambda)/(1-lambda) d mu_f(lambda)]
        / Var_pi_j(f).                                  (AI)

For a stationary trajectory, the exact covariance expansion gives

    Var[(1/M) sum_(t=0)^(M-1) f(X_t)]
      <= tau(f;K) Var_pi_j(f)/M.                         (AJ)

The inverse absolute gap bounds (AI) simultaneously for every `f`, but that
uniformity is unnecessary.  It is sufficient to prove the following weaker
clause, together with the same aggregate or matrix concentration step used in
the independent-sample Hessian argument:

> **Observable mobile-frame theorem.**  Construct a samplable generating
> family `V subset Lambda_0` such that, for every Walsh character needed by
> the final transform and every traceless symmetric `B`,
>
>     tau(f_(theta,B);K_V)
>       <= 2^((delta+o(1))n),
>
> with `delta<0.02648284...`.

The segmenter-style separation between orientation and phase gives a concrete
frame criterion.  A direction `v` acts on a tail character by

    (-1)^(theta.V_tail(x+kv))
      = (-1)^(theta.V_tail(x))
        (-1)^(k theta.V_tail(v)).                       (AIa)

Thus physical isotropy alone is insufficient.  The selected directions must
also have a well-conditioned binary incidence matrix: every nonzero `theta`
must pair oddly with enough *mobile* directions.  A natural construction
objective is therefore the minimum of two leverage scores:

1. Euclidean leverage of the normalized physical vectors, weighted by their
   one-dimensional Gaussian mobility;
2. mod-two character coverage, weighted by the same mobility.

This is the precise analogue of transporting both a local covector and its
relative phase.  It is directly checkable without diagonalizing the full
lattice chain.  The finite audit now reports the minimum unweighted and
mobility-weighted coverage over every nonzero tail character.

This is a strict weakening of (AC).  In particular, the Rademacher witness
used after (AF) can live on slow state distinctions to which all quadratic
Walsh observables are nearly orthogonal.  Such a witness proves a small
absolute gap but does not prove a large Hessian autocorrelation time.  The
mobile-shell threshold (AH) therefore remains a warning for a full mixing
theorem, not an impossibility result for recovery.

`experiments/walsh_line_heat_bath_kernel.py` now diagonalizes the finite
kernel once and evaluates (AI) on the complete span of the vectorized
traceless Hessian for every remaining Walsh character.  The audit is exact on
the truncated state space.  It separates three quantities that should no
longer be conflated: the absolute gap, the linear-observable gap, and the
Walsh--Hessian integrated autocorrelation.

The separation is material even in dimension four.  On one enumerated coset,
the physical-plus-Walsh frame has absolute gap `0.001982`, so the universal
spectral bound permits asymptotic variance inflation about `1008`.  The exact
worst inflation over the entire traceless-Hessian span and all four remaining
Walsh characters is only `21.72`, a factor `46.4` smaller.  The boundary mass
is `1.07e-4`.  This does not establish an exponent, but it proves that the
new clause is not merely a cosmetic reformulation of the absolute gap.

There are two possible analytic closures.  One can prove (AI) directly by a
low-degree comparison theorem for discrete Gaussians, or solve the observable
Poisson equation

    (I-K) g_(theta,B) = f_(theta,B)

and bound only the transported energy of `g`.  The latter is the exact
Markov-chain analogue of the repository's secant/preimage decomposition: it
does not reconstruct state in directions invisible to the requested output.

## 21. What the reversible transport hierarchy actually carries

Schur elimination by itself is not a sampler for a discrete Gaussian.  This
can be seen without an asymptotic estimate.  Choose coefficient coordinates
`z=(u,v)` for an affine lattice and write its quadratic action as

    E(u,v)
      = u^T A u + 2 u^T B v + v^T D v
        + 2 d^T u + 2 e^T v + c.

Completing the square in `u` gives the Schur action

    E_S(v)
      = v^T (D-B^T A^(-1)B) v
        + 2(e-B^T A^(-1)d)^T v + c-d^T A^(-1)d,

but the exact coarse marginal is

    pi_V(v)
      proportional to exp[-pi E_S(v)/s^2] Theta_A(m(v)),  (AK)

where

    m(v) = -A^(-1)(Bv+d),
    Theta_A(m)
      = sum_(u in Z^b)
          exp[-pi (u-m)^T A(u-m)/s^2].                  (AL)

The factor `Theta_A(m(v))` is the eliminated theta message.  It depends on
the coarse state through a torus-valued relative phase.  Keeping only the
Schur complement retains Gaussian action but discards exactly the information
needed to reconstruct the discrete marginal.

This matches the transport doctrine developed by the segmenter, FlowCells,
and the placement work:

    coarse mass/operator  = the Schur action E_S,
    connection/phase      = the conditional center m(v) mod Z^b,
    lifting detail        = the sampled fine residual u-m(v),
    missing message       = the theta normalizer Theta_A(m(v)).

Kron reduction and graph pyramids justify preserving the coarse quadratic
response and storing a prediction residual.  Sparse/decomposable transport
theory says that the theta message can remain local when the target has small
separators.  An arbitrary lattice Gram matrix generally has a complete
conditional-independence graph, however, so those results do not supply a
bounded separator for the worst-case lattice.  Approximate Gaussian
elimination and effective-resistance sparsification apply directly to
Laplacian/SDD quadratic forms; they do not by themselves control (AL).

The hierarchy is still useful in one exact form: do not marginalize a block.
For a low-rank sublattice `W`, the heat bath

    H_W f = E[f(X) | X mod W]

samples the lifting detail conditionally while keeping the quotient label.
It is an orthogonal projection and therefore discards no theta message.
Overlapping blocks progressively transport the detail between quotients.  If
rank-`b` shifted DGS costs `2^((kappa+o(1))n)` per update and the observable
relaxation exponent is `delta`, the actual budget is the tradeoff

    kappa + delta < 0.02648284... .                     (AM)

Thus the remaining viable target is smaller than a general sampler and more
precise than block tensorization for all functions: construct a line/block
transport hierarchy whose quotient phases are retained and prove (AI) only
for the Walsh--Hessian observable family, with the cost-action tradeoff (AM).

## 22. Stable lifting decomposition proves the observable bound

The reversible hierarchy yields an exact sufficient certificate.  Work in
the centered Hilbert space `L^2(pi_j)`.  Put

    P_i = I-H_(v_i),
    L = I-K_V = (1/m) sum_i P_i.

Every `P_i` is an orthogonal projection.  Suppose an observable `f` admits a
stable fiber decomposition

    f = sum_(i=1)^m g_i,    g_i in range(P_i),
    sum_i ||g_i||_pi^2 <= C ||f||_pi^2.                 (AN)

Then

    <f,L^+f>_pi <= m C ||f||_pi^2,                      (AO)

and hence

    tau(f;K_V) <= 2mC-1.                                (AP)

Indeed, the dual Dirichlet principle and `g_i=P_i g_i` give

    <f,L^+f>
      = sup_h {2<f,h>-<h,Lh>}
      = sup_h sum_i
          {2<g_i,P_i h>-(1/m)||P_i h||^2}
      <= m sum_i ||g_i||^2.

The transport hierarchy constructs (AN), rather than merely postulating it.
Order the directions and define one analysis sweep

    Q = H_(v_m) ... H_(v_1).

For a centered `f`, solve the one-sweep Poisson equation

    (I-Q)z=f.                                            (AQ)

Store its lifting details

    g_i
      = (I-H_(v_i)) H_(v_(i-1)) ... H_(v_1) z.          (AR)

Telescoping the sweep gives

    sum_i g_i = z-Qz=f,

and `g_i` lies in `range(P_i)`.  Therefore its exact stability coefficient

    C_lift(f)
      = sum_i ||g_i||_pi^2 / ||f||_pi^2                 (AS)

proves (AP).  This is the rigorous version of information living in
transport: the proof stores the detail removed by each conditional
projection, and synthesis of those details recovers exactly the requested
observable.  It never reconstructs an arbitrary state function.

The construction can also be written without a global inverse.  If repeated
sweeps make `Q^t f` tend to zero, take

    z = sum_(t>=0) Q^t f

and group the details from all sweeps by direction.  For example, a uniform
bound

    ||Q^t f|| <= q^t ||f||,    q<1,

on the observable orbit gives `C_lift(f)<=1/(1-q)^2`.  Thus any
`1-q=2^(-o(n))` ordered-sweep theorem proves a subexponential observable
relaxation bound.  The exact stability coefficient (AS) can be much sharper
when a very slow mode has only tiny overlap with `f`.

The finite audit now solves (AQ), forms every detail (AR), and verifies the
decomposition residual.  Across the enumerated three- and four-dimensional
cosets the residual is below `4e-15`.  For the difficult four-dimensional
physical-plus-Walsh frame with absolute gap `0.001982`, the exact
Walsh--Hessian inflation is `21.72` and the independently proved lifting-flow
bound is `23.76`; the gap-only bound is about `1008`.  The certificate captures
almost all of the observable improvement while ignoring the unrelated slow
mode.

The dimension-five audit gives the same qualitative result.  Across all four
enumerated half-kernel cosets, the `15`-direction physical-plus-Walsh frame has
exact worst observable inflation `11.47`--`27.94` and certified lifting bounds
`29.53`--`32.31`, with decomposition residual below `2e-15`.  Boundary mass is
below `3.0e-4`.  In contrast, the raw kernel generators reach exact inflation
`333.88`.  The stable frame is therefore tracking roughly the natural
`2|V|-1=29` random-scan scale through dimension five, while the arbitrary
generating set deteriorates rapidly.

The remaining geometric theorem can now be stated without a Poincare or
tensorization clause:

> **Random-half stable observable lifting theorem.**  In
> `2^(n/2+o(n))` preprocessing, construct and order a generating family
> `V subset Lambda_0`, `|V|=2^o(n)`, such that for every required
> Walsh--Hessian observable, the details (AR) satisfy
>
>     C_lift(f) <= 2^((delta+o(1))n)
>
> with `delta<0.02648284...` (and preferably `delta=0`).

Since `m=2^o(n)`, (AP) then gives exactly the autocorrelation exponent needed
by Section 20.  This theorem asks for stability of a concrete reversible
analysis/synthesis transform on a degree-two character family; it does not
ask the line walk to mix arbitrary functions.

## 23. Constant-rank blocks strengthen the certificate

Replace a line by a rank-`b` sublattice `W_i subset Lambda_0` and put

    H_(W_i) f = E[f(X) | X mod W_i].                     (AT)

This is again an orthogonal projection.  Sections 20 and 22 apply verbatim
with `H_(v_i)` replaced by `H_(W_i)`: the random-scan block kernel has

    L = (1/m) sum_i (I-H_(W_i)),

and an ordered sweep supplies the same stable lifting certificate.  Unlike a
line, a block transports interactions among several directions before taking
a quotient.  Its conditional update is a shifted rank-`b` DGS.  For constant
`b` and polynomially bounded geometric ratios, rigorous truncation and
fixed-dimensional enumeration cost `n^O(b)`, so the asymptotic transition
exponent remains zero.  The mobility clause below must also guarantee these
ratios; fixed rank alone is not a bit-complexity theorem.

The finite audit partitions the phase-aware direction frame into consecutive
rank-two and rank-three blocks and refreshes each entire block coset exactly.
The improvement is systematic:

- In dimension four, rank-two blocks reduce the exact worst observable
  inflation to `6.38`--`10.85`, with proved lifting bounds
  `11.00`--`11.52`.  Rank-three blocks reduce it to `2.91`--`6.93`, with
  proved bounds `7.00`--`7.08`.
- In dimension five, rank-two blocks give exact inflation
  `5.74`--`13.93`, with proved bounds `15.17`--`16.36`.  Rank-three blocks
  give `3.14`--`8.16`, with proved bounds `9.01`--`9.29`.

All decomposition residuals are at numerical roundoff.  The certified bounds
track the natural independent-block scale `2m-1`.  The twelve-direction frame
gives six rank-two or four rank-three blocks in dimension four; the
fifteen-direction frame gives eight rank-two or five rank-three blocks in
dimension five.  The observed certificates stay within a small constant of
`2 times the number of blocks minus 1`.

This does not yet prove that an efficiently constructed worst-case lattice
block has enough conditional Gaussian mobility.  It does establish that the
nonlinear/tensorization part is no longer the apparent bottleneck in the
tested systems: once a phase-aware mobile block frame is supplied, the
analysis/synthesis certificate is essentially tight and dimension-stable.

The geometric target should therefore be factored once more:

1. construct `O(n)` syndrome-zero directions, in the existing
   `2^(n/2+o(n))` preprocessing budget, whose physical spans and mod-two tail
   incidences are both well conditioned;
2. group them into overlapping constant-rank blocks whose shifted Gaussian
   conditionals have `2^(-o(n))` usable mass away from the current point;
3. prove that the ordered block sweep has stable detail energy
   `C_lift=2^o(n)` on the Walsh--Hessian family.

Clause 3 now has an exact algebraic certificate and strong finite evidence.
Clause 2 is the remaining mobility question.  It is weaker than asking for a
spanning shell of individually short directions: several directions may be
frozen alone while their block lattice has a mobile conditional population.

## 24. Mobility is a twisted-theta separation condition

The block mobility clause has a local Fourier formulation.  Let `W` be a
rank-`b` block lattice with basis rows `C=(v_1,...,v_b)`, and let `W#` denote
its dual inside `span(W)`.  Restrict a tail Walsh character to the block:

    chi_theta(sum_i k_i v_i) = (-1)^(beta.k),
    beta_i = theta.V_tail(v_i) mod 2.

Choose the half-dual representative `eta_beta in span(W)` satisfying

    <eta_beta,v_i> = beta_i/2 mod Z.

For a fiber shift `x`, define the twisted block theta function

    Z_beta(x)
      = sum_(w in W) rho_s(x+w) chi_theta(w).            (AU)

Poisson summation in `span(W)` gives, up to the common perpendicular Gaussian
factor and a unit phase,

    Z_beta(x)
      = (s^b/det W) sum_(y in W#)
          exp[-pi s^2 ||y-eta_beta||^2]
          exp[2 pi i <y,x_parallel>].                   (AV)

The untwisted denominator of the block conditional is the same expression
with `beta=0`.  Consequently the local character attenuation is governed by

    d_W(beta) = dist(eta_beta,W#).                       (AW)

The conditional first and second moments needed by the Hessian observable are
derivatives of (AV).  A shell bound for the shifted dual theta series,
including its degree-two polynomial moments, therefore gives a fully local
route to bounding `H_W f_(theta,B)`.

In coordinates, the distance is an explicit constant-rank CVP:

    d_W(beta)^2
      = min_(z in Z^b)
          (z+beta/2)^T (C C^T)^(-1) (z+beta/2).          (AX)

The experiment evaluates (AX) exactly for every block and every nonzero tail
character.  It reports

    min_(theta != 0) max_(blocks active on theta)
        s d_W(beta_theta),                               (AY)

together with the fraction of blocks active on the worst character.  On the
dimension-four fixture, the minimum best margin rises from `0.3420` for the
line frame to `0.4322` for rank-two blocks and `0.4514` for rank-three blocks;
the worst character is active on `0.50`, `0.83`, and `0.75` of the respective
blocks.  The improvement agrees with the exact autocorrelation and lifting
certificates only partially.  A second diagnostic prevents an incorrect
conclusion: the maximum untwisted block-dual theta tail rises from about
`1.41` for the line frame to `4.54` and `11.05` for the rank-two and
rank-three groupings.  The useful blocks are emphatically not becoming flat
in the ordinary smoothing sense.

What improves is the *normalized twisted response*, including cancellations
between dual terms.  The audit therefore also applies each exact block
projection to the full traceless-Hessian span for every character.  On the
same dimension-four coset, the best rank-three block contracts the worst
nonzero character/Hessian subspace by a factor at most `0.969` and the zero
character quadratic subspace by `0.681`; the complete ordered sweep is much
stronger than any one local block.

This initially suggested the following analytic closure for Clause 2 of
Section 23:

> **Local normalized twisted-theta block lemma (rejected universal target).**
> Construct an
> ordered constant-rank block cover such that every nonzero tail character
> has polynomial attenuation in some block for the normalized ratio
> `Z_beta(x)/Z_0(x)` and its first two source derivatives, uniformly over the
> relevant fiber shifts.  Require the untwisted quadratic channel to cover
> the physical traceless-Hessian directions as well.  Prove that composing
> these local projections gives `C_lift=2^o(n)`.

The separate flatness condition was deliberately removed from this target:
the finite results disprove it as an explanation of the block gain.  A merely
nonzero margin is also insufficient if the normalized denominator or
derivative moments are ill conditioned.  But all remaining local quantities
are ratios of theta series of constant-rank blocks, and the global arbitrary-
function mixing problem appeared to have disappeared from the proof target.

## 25. A constant-rank no-go theorem

The preceding target is false for worst-case lattices at `s=xi_r`.  The
obstruction is not a poor block order and is not repaired by normalizing the
theta functions.  A constant-rank slice contains only polynomially many
shell directions, while a cold discrete Gaussian on a dense high-dimensional
lattice stores its mass in exponentially many directions at once.

Here is the precise statement.  Let `Lambda_n` be a sequence of self-dual
Conway--Thompson lattices with

    lambda_1(Lambda_n)^2 >= (c^2-o(1)) n               (AZ)

for a fixed `c>0`.  Such a sequence exists; asymptotically one may take
`c^2=1/(2 pi e)`.  Put `L=Lambda_n`, so `L*=Lambda_n`, and use the paper's
target scale

    s^2 = xi_r^2
        = 4 n r ln(2)/(pi lambda_1(L)^2)
        = 4 r ln(2)/(pi c^2) + o(1).                   (BA)

Thus `s=Theta(1)`, whereas every nonzero vector of every sublattice of
`Lambda_n` has length `Omega(s sqrt(n))`.

Let `Lambda_0` be a good random half-kernel and let
`A=x_j+Lambda_0`.  The full-dual flatness estimate already proved at the
scale appearing in Section 13 says

    epsilon_n := rho_(1/s)(Lambda_0* \ {0}) = 2^(-Omega(n)).  (BB)

Consequently Poisson summation gives, uniformly in the real shift `t`,

    rho_s(A+t)/rho_s(A) <= (1+epsilon_n)/(1-epsilon_n). (BC)

Fix any rank-`b` block `W subset Lambda_0`, where `b` is independent of `n`,
and let `H_W` be its exact heat-bath projection.  If `X~D_(A,s)` and `X'` is
one `H_W` refresh, then

    Pr[X' != X]
      <= sum_(w in W\{0})
           sum_(x in A) min(rho_s(x),rho_s(x+w))/rho_s(A)
      <= (1+epsilon_n)/(1-epsilon_n)
           sum_(w in W\{0}) exp(-pi ||w||^2/(4s^2)).   (BD)

The second inequality uses

    min(rho_s(x),rho_s(x+w))
      <= sqrt(rho_s(x)rho_s(x+w))
      = exp(-pi||w||^2/(4s^2)) rho_s(x+w/2).            (BE)

Packing disjoint radius-`lambda_1(W)/2` balls inside `span(W)` shows that
the number of block vectors of length at most `k lambda_1(W)` is at most
`(2k+1)^b`.  Equations (AZ)--(BE) therefore imply

    Pr[X' != X]
      <= 2^(-(delta_CT(r)-o(1))n),
    delta_CT(r) = pi c^2/(4s^2 ln 2) > 0.              (BF)

The same conclusion holds for every `b=o(n)`, since the packing factor is
then only `2^o(n)` at the Gaussian-dominant shells.

For the asymptotic Conway--Thompson value `c^2=1/(2 pi e)`, this is

    delta_CT(r) = 1/(64 e^2 r (ln 2)^2).               (BG)

At the current `r=0.23675858...`, `s^2=3.568746...` and
`delta_CT=0.0185898...`.  The exact number is secondary: it is a positive
constant, not `o(1)`.

This already contradicts polynomial local attenuation.  On a block fiber,

    Z_beta(x)/Z_0(x) = E[chi_beta(X'-X) | X=x].         (BH)

If the conditional escape probability is `a_W(x)`, then

    |1-Z_beta(x)/Z_0(x)| <= 2 a_W(x).                  (BI)

By (BF) and Markov, for a stationary-typical fiber the degree-zero normalized
response of every one of `m=2^o(n)` fixed-rank blocks is
`1-2^(-Omega(n))`.  Hence it cannot be attenuated by `1/poly(n)`.  The
failure is already in the zeroth theta jet, so adding its first two
derivatives cannot rescue the proposed conjunctive condition.

There is also a direct lifting-stability lower bound.  Put

    L_block = (1/m) sum_i (I-H_(W_i)).                 (BJ)

Choose a bounded, centered, zero-Walsh traceless quadratic feature `f` from
the truncated Hessian family.  Flatness and its degree-four differentiated
form give `||f||_2^2 >= n^(-O(1))`, while the truncation gives
`||f||_infinity <= n^O(1)`.  Equations (BD)--(BF) imply

    <f,L_block f>/||f||_2^2
      <= 2^(-(delta_CT(r)-o(1))n).                     (BK)

If `f` is not in the range of `L_block`, the requested decomposition does
not exist.  Otherwise spectral Cauchy--Schwarz gives

    <f,L_block^+ f> <f,L_block f> >= ||f||_2^4.        (BL)

But any lifting decomposition with detail constant `C_lift` satisfies the
proved upper bound

    <f,L_block^+ f> <= m C_lift ||f||_2^2.             (BM)

Combining (BK)--(BM) yields

    C_lift >= 2^((delta_CT(r)-o(1))n).                 (BN)

No ordering changes this lower bound, because (BJ) depends only on the block
family.  Thus an ordered `2^o(n)`-size, constant-rank syndrome-zero block
cover cannot have polynomial theta-jet attenuation or `2^o(n)` lifting-detail
stability uniformly over the lattices in the source theorem.

The viable target must relax one of the two structural restrictions:

1. use blocks of rank `Omega(n)` so that one conditional contains
   exponentially many competing directions;
2. use a genuinely nonlocal transport whose proposal is controlled by the
   full dual theta mass rather than a fixed low-rank slice; or
3. retain constant-rank blocks but prove only an exponential constant below
   the recovery budget, not exponent zero.

The last possibility is not vacuous: the Conway--Thompson overlap exponent
`0.01859...` is below the current allowance `0.02648...`.  It leaves a narrow
constant-improvement theorem to chase, but it rules out the requested
polynomial/`2^o(n)` theorem.
